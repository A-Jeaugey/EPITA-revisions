Electronics B2: Thévenin, Norton, Millman

The rest of the circuit analysis tools course, in DC circuits: reduce a circuit to one source and one resistor seen from two terminals, write the voltage of a node in one line, split a circuit source by source. This is the syllabus of the end-of-semester exam (45% of the grade), with a course MCQ on the whole semester.

The essentials

Six skills to master on exam day

  1. Turn off the sourcesVoltage source → wire, current source → gap. This is the basis of Rth and of superposition, and an MCQ question.
    Course →
  2. Transform a source(E, R) in series ⇄ (E/R, R) in parallel, copying the direction of the arrows.
    Course →
  3. Reduce in a chainBranches in parallel → Norton to add the currents; elements in series → Thévenin to add the emfs. One diagram per step.
    Method →
  4. Spot what does not matterA resistor in series with a current source, a resistor in parallel with a voltage source.
    Course →
  5. Write Millman in one goGround at B, one contribution per branch, current sources in the numerator only.
    Course →
  6. Split by superpositionOne state per source, contributions with their sign, numerical application at the end.
    Course →
