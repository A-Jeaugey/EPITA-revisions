In the exam

The paper almost always names the method in the title of the exercise (“Norton’s theorem”). Use that one: the marking scheme follows its steps. When it does not name one, method 6 helps you choose.

Method 1

Direct Thévenin

  1. Disconnect the load (the resistor “seen by”) and mark its two terminals A and B.
  2. Eth = open-circuit voltage between A and B: voltage divider, Millman or superposition. No current leaves through A.
  3. Rth: turn off the sources (voltage → wire, current → gap), then the resistance seen from A and B.
  4. Reconnect the load: U = Eth·RL/(Rth + RL).
Quick example

Eth = 12 × 6/(3 + 6) = 8 V; Rth = 3 // 6 = 2 kΩ; with RL = 2 kΩ: U = 8 × 2/4 = 4 V.

In the exam

January 2018, 2019, 2020 and 2021: “Thévenin generator seen by R′ (or R4)”, 5 to 6.5 points. Practice

Method 2

Direct Norton

  1. Disconnect the load, then join A and B with a wire.
  2. IN = short-circuit current in that wire, from B to A through the generator. Resistors in parallel with the wire are short-circuited: they no longer count.
  3. RN = Rth: remove the wire, turn off the sources, resistance seen from A and B.
  4. Reconnect the load: current divider, I = IN·RN/(RN + RL), the other resistor on top.
Quick example

Same circuit: wire between A and B, the 6 kΩ is short-circuited, IN = 12/3 = 4 mA; RN = 2 kΩ; with RL = 2 kΩ: I = 4 × 2/(2 + 2) = 2 mA, so U = 2 × 2 = 4 V, as with Thévenin.

Check: Eth = RN·IN = 2 × 4 = 8 V.

In the exam

January 2016, 2017, 2020, 2021, 2022 and 2023: “Norton seen by…”, then the current in the load. The most frequent trap is the current divider upside down. Practice

Method 3

Chained Thévenin ⇄ Norton transformations

This is the main exercise of the 2022 and 2023 papers (12 and 14 points) and of the 2023 and 2024 midterms. The marker expects one intermediate diagram per transformation.

  1. Disconnect the load. Everything that remains is the “generator” to simplify.
  2. Remove what does not matter: a resistor in series with a current source, a resistor in parallel with a voltage source (course, part 7).
  3. Branches in parallel between the same two nodes: turn each series (E, R) into a Norton (E/R // R, arrow toward the +), then add the current sources (algebraically) and combine the parallel resistors.
  4. Elements one after another on a path: turn each (I // R) into a Thévenin (RI in series with R, emf in the direction of I), then add the emfs (algebraically, from B to A) and the resistances.
  5. “(E, R) loaded by a resistor” block: intermediate Thévenin, E·R₂/(R₁ + R₂) in series with R₁ // R₂.
  6. A single source and a single resistor: that is (Eth, Rth) or (IN, RN), depending on the form asked. Reconnect the load, divider.
  7. Check Rth on the original circuit, sources turned off, and the units (V everywhere, RI is in V; A everywhere, E/R is in A).

The arrow rule

Norton: the current arrow points toward the + terminal of the voltage source it replaces. Thévenin: the emf arrow points in the direction of the arrow of the current it replaces. A current source that leaves node A counts negatively.

In the exam

In 2022, question 2 is done by turning all the branches into Norton generators: they are all in parallel between the same two nodes, and U comes out of a single fraction. Practice

Method 4

Millman in three steps

  1. Ground at the common point of the sources (often the bottom wire). Name the node you want A.
  2. Numerator: for each branch arriving at A, (potential of the other end ± emf)/R, + if the emf points toward A; plus the currents of the current sources (+ entering, − leaving).
  3. Denominator: sum of the 1/R of the resistor branches, without those of the current-source branches (their series resistance does not count). Multiply top and bottom so that you have a single fraction bar.
Quick example

VA = (12/2 + 3)/(1/2 + 1/2) = 9/1 = 9 V

The 3 A source adds its current to the numerator and nothing to the denominator.

In the exam

As a bonus in 2016, 2017 and 2019, graded 2 points in 2018; in 2017, the whole of exercise 3 is done with Millman (then “what value of R4 gives U = 0?”). Practice

Method 5

Superposition: one state per source

  1. Count the sources: as many states as sources (three sources, three diagrams).
  2. In each state, keep a single source and turn off the others: voltage → wire, current → gap. Redraw the simplified circuit.
  3. Compute the contribution with a voltage or current divider, with its sign relative to the arrow of the quantity asked.
  4. Add the contributions, and only then do the numerical application.
Quick example

Same circuit as Millman. 12 V alone (3 A → gap): VA′ = 12 × 2/(2 + 2) = 6 V. 3 A alone (12 V → wire): VA″ = 3 × (2 // 2) = 3 V. Total: 6 + 3 = 9 V, as with Millman.

Watch out

Superposition adds voltages and currents, never powers. And a source that is turned off stays in the circuit as a wire or a gap: do not erase it together with its resistor.

In the exam

June 2018, January 2020 and January 2021, often with a numerical application at the end (4.5 to 7.5 points). Practice

Method 6

“Find the voltage across R1”: which method?

January 2016, June 2018, January 2018 and 2019 ask this question without imposing a method, on circuits with two or three sources (7 to 8 points). The shortest path:

What you seeMethodWhy
R1 between two nodes, the rest reduces nicelyThévenin seen by R1A single reduction, then a voltage divider.
All the branches between the same two nodesMillmanA single fraction, no transformation.
Sources of different types, well separatedSuperpositionEach state is a simple divider.

A source disappears?

It can happen and it is not a mistake. In January 2019, E does not appear in U = 6RR1I/(4R + R1); in June 2018, it is I that disappears. Check with superposition: the contribution of that source is 0.

Method 7

The numerical application without a calculator

  1. Symbolic expression first, simplified, boxed: it earns most of the points.
  2. Consistent units: resistances in kΩ, currents in mA, voltages in V (kΩ × mA = V, V / kΩ = mA).
  3. Substitute in the final expression only, not at every step.
  4. Keep the sign: −1.5 A is a good answer (January 2020) when the arrow points the other way.

Method 8

Check without a calculator

Units

Every term of a sum has the same unit: RI and E are volts, E/R and I are amperes. A result U = E + I is certainly wrong.

Limiting cases

Set I = 0 or E = 0 and check that the result matches the simplified circuit. A load tending to infinity gives Eth back; a zero load gives IN.

Rth twice

Once through the transformations, once by turning off the sources on the original diagram. If the two differ, an arrow or a grouping is wrong.

Another method

Superposition or Millman at node A give the same result in a few lines. And Eth = RN·IN links the two generators.

To avoid

The 14 traps found in the papers

  1. Keeping the load in Eth or RthDisconnect it first, reconnect it at the end (2021, 2023).
  2. Short-circuiting a current source for RthA current source turned off = gap. In 2019, you would find R/2 instead of 2R/3.
  3. Counting a resistor in series with a current sourceIt changes neither the current nor Rth (2018 Ex 2, Millman 2017 and 2019).
  4. Keeping a resistor in parallel with a voltage sourceWhen the source is turned off (wire), it is short-circuited (2020 Ex 4).
  5. Current divider upside downIt is the resistance of the other branch that goes in the numerator (2016, 2022).
  6. Reading a source arrow backwards2018 Ex 3: the 2E opposes the direction of travel; 2021 Ex 4: I0 goes to the left, RI0 is subtracted.
  7. Forgetting that a current source draws current2023: I leaves the top node, hence E/(16R) − I.
  8. Forgetting a resistor on the bottom wire2017 (R4), 2023 (R in series with 3R), June 2018 (2R).
  9. Adding a current source and a series resistorWithout a transformation, it makes no sense (2022).
  10. Forgetting a superposition stateThree sources, three states (2020 Ex 4).
  11. Losing a sign in a state2021 Ex 2: with I0 alone, the current goes through 3R against the I2 arrow.
  12. Putting 1/R of a current-source branch in the Millman denominator2017, 2018, 2019: its resistance does not count.
  13. Copying Eth as the answerJune 2018: they ask for U across the load (denominator 14), not the open-circuit voltage (denominator 11). The official answer key gets it wrong.
  14. Mixing Ω and kΩ2017, 2021: everything in kΩ and mA, or everything in Ω and A.
Exam day

Writing your paper

No calculator or documents, answers directly on the paper (it gives no duration). It starts with a course MCQ (5 to 8 points), then two or three theorem exercises. What earns points:

  • The named theorem at the start of each answer: “Thévenin seen by R′”, “Norton → Thévenin”, “superposition, state 1”.
  • One diagram per step: load disconnected, sources turned off, each transformation, each superposition state.
  • The symbolic expression first, then the numerical value with its unit.
  • The result boxed, simplified (a single fraction bar).
  • The MCQ: it covers the whole semester, B1 laws included (measuring instruments, units, equivalent resistances, dividers). Revise it with the Elec B2 MCQ and the B1 one.
