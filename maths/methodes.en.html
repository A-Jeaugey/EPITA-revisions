Method 1 · OBM LE 2023 ex. 2 · OBM 2024 ex. 1.2 · lesson 1, part 7

Negating a quantified statement

  1. Swap each quantifier: \(\forall\) becomes \(\exists\), \(\exists\) becomes \(\forall\). The set that follows does not change: \(\forall x \in \R^+\) becomes \(\exists x \in \R^+\).

  2. Keep the order of the quantifiers and of the variables.

  3. Negate the final property: \(=\) becomes \(\neq\), \(\leq\) becomes \(>\), “and” becomes “or” (and conversely), \(P \Rightarrow Q\) becomes \(P \land \neg Q\). A double inequality \(m \leq f(x) \leq M\) becomes \(f(x) < m\) or \(f(x) > M\).

  4. True or false? A true \(\forall\) is proved (“Let \(x \in \R\)”); a false \(\forall\) is disproved by a counterexample with numbers; a true \(\exists\) is proved by a witness.

Example · OBM exam 2024, exercise 1.2

\(P\): “\(\forall x \in \R, \exists y \in \R, \cos(x) = y\)”, \(Q\): “\(\exists y \in \R, \forall x \in \R, x = \cos(y)\)”, \(R\): “\(\exists x \in \R, \exists y \in \R, \cos(x) < \cos(y)\)”. Are they true? Give their negations.

Model answer

\(P\) is true: let \(x \in \R\); \(y = \cos(x)\) works.

\(Q\) is false: let \(y \in \R\); for \(x = \cos(y) + 1\), \(x \neq \cos(y)\). No \(y\) works for all \(x\).

\(R\) is true: for \(x = \pi\) and \(y = 0\), \(\cos(\pi) = -1 < 1 = \cos(0)\).

\(\neg P\): \(\exists x \in \R, \forall y \in \R, \cos(x) \neq y\).

\(\neg Q\): \(\forall y \in \R, \exists x \in \R, x \neq \cos(y)\).

\(\neg R\): \(\forall x \in \R, \forall y \in \R, \cos(x) \geq \cos(y)\).

LE 2023, exercise 2.2

\(R\): “\((\forall x \in \R, f(x) > 0) \lor (\forall x \in \R, f(x) < 0)\)” has the negation \((\exists x \in \R, f(x) \leq 0) \land (\exists x \in \R, f(x) \geq 0)\): De Morgan on the “or”, then each block negated.

Traps
  • Changing the set: the negation of \(\forall x \in \R^+, f(x) \geq 0\) is \(\exists x \in \R^+, f(x) < 0\), not \(\exists x \in \R^-\).
  • Negating \(\leq\) into \(<\): the negation of \(f(x) \leq M\) is \(f(x) > M\).
  • Swapping the quantifiers: \(\forall x\,\exists y\) (\(y\) depends on \(x\)) and \(\exists y\,\forall x\) (one \(y\) for all) do not mean the same thing.
Method 2 · OBM OBM 2024 ex. 1.1 · LE 2023 ex. 2.2b · lesson 1, part 4

Necessary and sufficient conditions: placing ⇒, ⇐ or ⇔

  1. Write the set of \(x\) satisfying each condition (solve).

  2. Compare the sets: if the left one is included in the right one, the implication \(\text{left} \Rightarrow \text{right}\) is true. Equal: \(\Leftrightarrow\).

  3. Justify a false implication with a counterexample: an \(x\) satisfying the left side but not the right one.

  4. Vocabulary: if \(P \Rightarrow Q\), \(P\) is a sufficient condition for \(Q\) and \(Q\) a necessary condition for \(P\).

Example · OBM exam 2024, exercise 1.1

\(P\): “\(x^2 > 4\)”. Propositions: (1) \(x > -2\); (2) \(x < -10\); (3) \(x > 1.9\); (4) \(x < -3\) or \(x > 3\); (5) \(x < -2\) or \(x > 2\). Which implications “… ⇒ P” are true? Which “P ⇒ …”? Negate the true implications of the second type.

Model answer

\(x^2 > 4 \iff x \in \left]-\infty, -2\right[ \cup \left]2, +\infty\right[\). The sets of the propositions are \(\left]-2, +\infty\right[\), \(\left]-\infty, -10\right[\), \(\left]1.9, +\infty\right[\), \(\left]-\infty, -3\right[ \cup \left]3, +\infty\right[\) and \(\left]-\infty, -2\right[ \cup \left]2, +\infty\right[\).

a) True: (2) ⇒ P, (4) ⇒ P and (5) ⇒ P (inclusions). False: (1) ⇒ P (\(x = 0\)) and (3) ⇒ P (\(x = 2\), \(2^2 = 4\) is not \(> 4\)).

b) Only P ⇒ (5) is true (equal sets). P ⇒ (1), P ⇒ (3) are false (\(x = -3\)); P ⇒ (2), P ⇒ (4) are false (\(x = 2.5\), \(2.5^2 = 6.25\)).

c) The negation of “P ⇒ (5)” is “\(x^2 > 4\) and \(-2 \leq x \leq 2\)”.

Traps
  • The arrow goes from the small set to the large one: \(x > 3 \Rightarrow x^2 > 4\), not the converse.
  • The negation of an implication is not an implication: \(\neg(P \Rightarrow Q) \iff P \land \neg Q\).
Method 3 · OBM OBM 2024 ex. 1.3 and 1.4 · OBM 2025 ex. 1.4 · lesson 4, parts 3 and 4

Contrapositive and double inclusion

  1. Contrapositive of \(A \Rightarrow B\): \(\neg B \Rightarrow \neg A\). It is equivalent: \((A \Rightarrow B) \iff (\neg A \lor B) \iff (B \lor \neg A) \iff (\neg B \Rightarrow \neg A)\), or a truth table. The converse \(B \Rightarrow A\) is not.

  2. Proof by contrapositive: write the contrapositive in full, then “Assume \(\neg B\)” and reach \(\neg A\).

  3. Proving \(A \subset B\): first line “Let \(x \in A\) be given.”, then show \(x \in B\). For \(A = B\): both inclusions. For \(B \not\subset A\): an element of \(B\) not in \(A\).

Example · OBM exam 2024, exercise 1.3 (c) and (d)

Model answer

We want to show \((\forall \varepsilon > 0, |a| \leq \varepsilon) \Rightarrow a = 0\). Its contrapositive is: \(a \neq 0 \Rightarrow \exists \varepsilon > 0, |a| > \varepsilon\).

Assume \(a \neq 0\). Let \(\varepsilon = \frac{|a|}{2}\). Then \(\varepsilon > 0\) and \(|a| > \frac{|a|}{2} = \varepsilon\). The contrapositive is true, so the implication is too.

(d) The converse is true: if \(a = 0\), for all \(\varepsilon > 0\), \(|a| = 0 \leq \varepsilon\).

Example · OBM exam 2024, exercise 1.4

Model answer

\(A = \{(x, y, z) \in \R^3,\ x = y = z\}\) and \(B = \{(x, y, z) \in \R^3,\ 2x - y - z = 0\}\) are subsets of \(\R^3\).

Let \((x, y, z) \in A\) be given. Then \(y = x\) and \(z = x\), so \(2x - y - z = 2x - x - x = 0\): \((x, y, z) \in B\). Thus \(A \subset B\).

\(B \not\subset A\): \(2 \times 1 - 2 - 0 = 0\), so \((1, 2, 0) \in B\), but \(1 \neq 2\), so \((1, 2, 0) \notin A\).

Traps
  • Mixing up the contrapositive (\(\neg B \Rightarrow \neg A\), equivalent) and the converse (\(B \Rightarrow A\)).
  • Proving an inclusion on an example: you need an arbitrary element (“Let … be given”).
Method 4 · OBM LE 2023 ex. 3 · OBM 2024 ex. 2 and 3 · lesson 3, parts 2 and 3

Direct images and pre-images

Definitions asked in every OBM exam

Let \(f : E \to F\), \(A \subset E\) and \(B \subset F\). \(f(A) = \{f(x),\ x \in A\} = \{y \in F,\ \exists x \in A,\ y = f(x)\}\), a subset of \(F\). \(f^{-1}(B) = \{x \in E,\ f(x) \in B\}\), a subset of \(E\), defined even if \(f\) is not bijective.

  1. Finite set: tabulate \(f\). \(f(A)\): the values taken on \(A\). \(f^{-1}(B)\): the elements of the input set whose image is in \(B\); it may be empty.

  2. Interval: draw the graph on the input set, cut it into pieces where it is strictly monotonic, take the values at the endpoints. An endpoint is closed if it is reached, open otherwise.

  3. Pre-image: solve \(f(x) \in B\) in the input set only.

Example · OBM exam 2024, exercise 2: \(f : E \times F \to \N\), \((x, y) \mapsto x + y\), \(E = \{2, 3, 4\}\), \(F = \{1, 2, 3\}\)

Model answer

Table: \(f(2, 1) = 3\), \(f(2, 2) = 4\), \(f(2, 3) = 5\), \(f(3, 1) = 4\), \(f(3, 2) = 5\), \(f(3, 3) = 6\), \(f(4, 1) = 5\), \(f(4, 2) = 6\), \(f(4, 3) = 7\).

\(f(E \times F) = \{3, 4, 5, 6, 7\}\); \(f(\{(2, 2), (4, 3)\}) = \{4, 7\}\); \(f^{-1}(\{1\}) = \varnothing\); \(f^{-1}(\{6\}) = \{(3, 3), (4, 2)\}\).

Example · OBM exam 2024, exercise 3: complete the table

\(\exp : \R \to \R\)\(\cos : [0, \pi] \to \R\)
\(f(\{0\})\)\(\{1\}\)\(\{1\}\)
\(f([0, \frac{\pi}{2}])\)\([1, e^{\pi/2}]\)\([0, 1]\)
\(f^{-1}(\{0\})\)\(\varnothing\)\(\{\frac{\pi}{2}\}\)
\(f^{-1}(\{-1\})\)\(\varnothing\)\(\{\pi\}\)
\(f^{-1}(]-\infty, 0])\)\(\varnothing\)\([\frac{\pi}{2}, \pi]\)

Draw \(f : \{a, b, c, d\} \to \ent{1}{5}\) such that \(f(\{a, b\}) = \{1, 2\}\), \(f^{-1}(\{5\}) = \varnothing\) and \(f^{-1}(\{2\}) = \{b, c\}\). First place what is imposed: \(f(b) = f(c) = 2\), so \(f(a) = 1\); \(f(d)\) is neither 2 nor 5: 1, 3 or 4. Exactly 3 functions work, for example:

Traps
  • Endpoints: \(\exp(]0, \pi]) = ]1, e^{\pi}]\), since 0 is excluded so 1 is too.
  • The input set: \(\sin^{-1}([0, 1])\) on \([-\pi, \pi]\) is \(\{-\pi\} \cup [0, \pi]\), because \(\sin(-\pi) = 0\).
  • \(f^{-1}(B)\) is a set, not the inverse function; \(f^{-1}(\{y\}) = \varnothing\) is possible.
Method 5 · OBM LE 2023 ex. 3.3 · OBM 2024 ex. 2.2 · lesson 3, parts 4 to 6

Injective, surjective, then restricting

Definitions

\(f : E \to F\) is injective if \(\forall (x, x') \in E^2,\ f(x) = f(x') \Rightarrow x = x'\); surjective if \(\forall y \in F,\ \exists x \in E,\ y = f(x)\); bijective if it is both.

  1. Not injective: two distinct elements with the same image, with numbers. Injective: “Let \((x, x') \in E^2\) such that \(f(x) = f(x')\)”, then reach \(x = x'\); or “strictly monotonic on the interval”.

  2. Not surjective: a value of \(F\) with no pre-image. Surjective: “Let \(y \in F\)”, then set an \(x\) and check \(f(x) = y\).

  3. Restricting: to make \(f\) injective, reduce the input set to an interval where it is strictly monotonic; to make it surjective, reduce the output set to the image \(f(E)\). Always give both sets and the reason.

Example · LE exam 2023, exercise 3.3: \(g : \R \to \R\), \(x \mapsto |x - 1|\)

Model answer

(c) \(g\) is not injective: \(g(0) = |0 - 1| = 1\) and \(g(2) = |2 - 1| = 1\), with \(0 \neq 2\). On \(I_1 = [1, +\infty[\), \(g(x) = x - 1\) is strictly increasing: \(g : I_1 \to J_1 = \R\) is injective.

(d) \(g\) is not surjective: for all \(x \in \R\), \(g(x) \geq 0\), so \(-2\) has no pre-image. \(g : I_2 = \R \to J_2 = \R^+\) is surjective: let \(y \geq 0\); for \(x = y + 1\), \(g(x) = |y| = y\).

Example · OBM exam 2024, exercise 2.2 (c) to (f): \(f(x, y) = x + y\) on \(E \times F\)

Model answer

(c) \(f\) is not injective: \(f(2, 2) = f(3, 1) = 4\) and \((2, 2) \neq (3, 1)\). (d) It is not surjective onto \(\N\): 0 has no pre-image (all the images are \(\geq 3\)).

(e) \(I = \{(2, 1), (2, 2)\}\), \(J = \{3, 4, 5\}\): images 3 and 4 are distinct, 5 has no pre-image. (f) \(I' = \{(2, 1), (2, 2), (3, 1)\}\), \(J' = \{3, 4\}\): 3 and 4 are reached, and \(f(2, 2) = f(3, 1)\).

Traps
  • “Increasing so injective”: it must be strictly monotonic.
  • Surjectivity depends on the output set: \(x \mapsto x^2\) is surjective from \(\R\) onto \(\R^+\), not onto \(\R\).
  • A restriction given without both sets, or without a reason.
Method 6 · OBM OBM 2025 and 2024 ex. 4 · LE 2023 ex. 4 · lesson 4, part 6 · not examined in 2026

Binary relations: the four properties

Not on this year’s syllabus

On 19 October 2026 your teacher wrote that the relations exercise of the 2025 paper is no longer on the syllabus. This method stays for the past papers; seminar exercise 6.27 is redone without this vocabulary (lesson 4, part 6).

Definitions from the 2022 and 2023 answer keys
  • \(\mathcal{R}\) reflexive: \(\forall x \in E,\ x \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} x\).
  • symmetric: \(\forall (x, y) \in E^2,\ x \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} y \Rightarrow y \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} x\).
  • antisymmetric: \(\forall (x, y) \in E^2,\ (x \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} y \text{ and } y \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} x) \Rightarrow x = y\).
  • transitive: \(\forall (x, y, z) \in E^3,\ (x \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} y \text{ and } y \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} z) \Rightarrow x \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} z\).
  • Order relation: reflexive, antisymmetric, transitive. Equivalence relation: reflexive, symmetric, transitive.

  1. Recall the definition before each property (OBM 2024: “After recalling the definition”).

  2. True property: “Let … be given”, then the proof. False: a counterexample with numbers, which satisfies the hypothesis and not the conclusion.

  3. Conclude: order relation, equivalence relation, or neither.

Example · OBM exam 2024, exercise 4: on \(\R\), \(a \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} b \iff |a| \leq |b|\)

Model answer

Reflexive: let \(a \in \R\); \(|a| \leq |a|\), so \(a \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} a\).

Not symmetric: \(1 \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} 2\) since \(1 \leq 2\), but \(2 \leq 1\) is false: we do not have \(2 \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} 1\).

Not antisymmetric: \(-1 \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} 1\) and \(1 \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} -1\) (since \(|-1| = |1|\)), but \(-1 \neq 1\).

Transitive: let \(a, b, c\) be real numbers with \(|a| \leq |b|\) and \(|b| \leq |c|\); then \(|a| \leq |c|\).

It is neither an order relation nor an equivalence relation.

LE 2023 · \(a \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} b \iff \exists n \in \N,\ b = a^n\) on \(\N^*\)

Reflexive (\(a = a^1\)), not symmetric (\(8 = 2^3\), so \(2 \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} 8\), but 2 is not a power of 8), transitive (\(c = b^p = a^{np}\)), antisymmetric (question 4): it is an order relation, which the answer key does not say.

Traps
  • Antisymmetric is not the opposite of symmetric: a relation may be neither (above).
  • Negations: not reflexive \(\exists x \in E,\ \neg(x \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} x)\); not antisymmetric \(\exists (x, y) \in E^2,\ x \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} y,\ y \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} x \text{ and } x \neq y\).
Method 7 · DP DP 2023 ex. 2 · DP 2024 ex. 1 and 2 · lesson 5, part 6

Choosing the counting model

  1. Does the order matter? Podium, code, distinct positions, successive draws: yes. Group, hand, simultaneous draw: no.

  2. Repetition allowed? With replacement, repeated digits: \(n^p\). Without: \(A_n^p = \frac{n!}{(n-p)!}\), or \(n!\) if everything is placed. Without order: \(\binom{n}{p} = \frac{n!}{p!\,(n-p)!}\).

  3. Combined cases: “at least” through the complement; “X but not Y”: place X, then complete without Y; “exactly”: product of the choices in each category; several disjoint cases: sum.

  4. Write one sentence per factor and give the result in the requested form (product, whole number).

Example · DP exam 2023, exercise 2: 20 cyclists, including Pierre and Jade

Model answer
  1. The order matters and a cyclist has only one place: 20 choices for the 1st, 19 for the 2nd, 18 for the 3rd, so \(20 \times 19 \times 18 = 6{,}840\) podiums.
  2. Jade is first: \(19 \times 18 = 342\).
  3. Without Pierre and without Jade: 18 cyclists, \(18 \times 17 \times 16 = 4{,}896\).
  4. Pierre but not Jade: Pierre has 3 places, then 18 and 17 choices among the others: \(3 \times 18 \times 17 = 918\).
  5. At least Jade or Pierre: through the complement, \(20 \times 19 \times 18 - 18 \times 17 \times 16 = 1{,}944\).
  6. The three bikes are identical: the order no longer matters, \(\binom{20}{3} = \frac{20 \times 19 \times 18}{3!} = 1{,}140\).
DP 2024, exercise 2 · door code: one letter out of A, B, C, then 3 digits

Distinct digits: \(3 \times A_{10}^{3} = 3 \times 720 = 2{,}160\). Repetitions allowed: \(3 \times 10^3 = 3{,}000\). Known letter, distinct odd digits: \(A_5^3 = 60\). Digits 6, 2, 9 in an unknown order, unknown letter: \(3 \times 3! = 18\). At least one 4: \(3 \times (10^3 - 9^3) = 813\).

Traps
  • Podium versus identical bikes: \(A_{20}^3\) against \(\binom{20}{3}\). Distinct positions versus “two assistants”.
  • With replacement: \(n^p\), neither \(A_n^p\) nor \(p^n\).
  • “At least one” counted as \(\binom{\cdot}{1} \times \dots\): the cases with several are counted twice. Go through the complement.
  • Anagrams with repeated letters: divide by the factorials of the repetitions (“tableau”: \(\frac{7!}{2!} = 2{,}520\)).
Method 8 · DP DP 2023 ex. 3 · DP 2024 ex. 3 · lesson 5, parts 7 and 8

An identity by double counting

  1. Name the set that both sides count (the draws of \(k\) balls, the token configurations).

  2. Count it in one go: that is one side.

  3. Count it in steps or by cases: that is the other side. For a sum, say the cases are disjoint and cover everything.

  4. Conclude: “we counted the same set in two ways”. If asked, prove it again by computation, with factorials.

Example · DP exam 2023, exercise 3: \(p\) green balls, \(n\) red ones, \(k\) balls drawn simultaneously

Model answer

There are \(\binom{n+p}{k}\) possible draws. For \(i \in \ent{0}{k}\), there are \(\binom{p}{i}\binom{n}{k-i}\) draws with \(i\) green and \(k - i\) red balls.

To take \(k\) balls, we can take 0 green and \(k\) red ones, or 1 green and \(k - 1\) red ones, …, up to \(k\) green and 0 red. These different cases are disjoint and cover all the draws. Hence \(\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k} \binom{p}{i}\binom{n}{k-i} = \binom{n+p}{k}\).

Application: \(\sum_{i=0}^{4} \binom{4}{i}\binom{7}{4-i} = \binom{11}{4} = 330\).

Example · DP exam 2024, exercise 3: 9 tokens, 5 in the hexagon including 2 in the triangle

Model answer

Choice 1: 2 tokens for the triangle, then 3 of the 7 left: \(N_1 = \binom{9}{2}\binom{7}{3} = 36 \times 35 = 1{,}260\). Choice 2: 5 tokens for the hexagon, then 2 of them for the triangle: \(N_2 = \binom{9}{5}\binom{5}{2} = 126 \times 10 = 1{,}260\). We count the same configurations, hence \(N_1 = N_2\).

In general, \(\binom{n}{p}\binom{p}{k} = \binom{n}{k}\binom{n-k}{p-k}\): both sides equal \(\frac{n!}{k!\,(p-k)!\,(n-p)!}\). Then \(S_p = \sum_{k=0}^{p} \binom{n}{k}\binom{n-k}{p-k} = \binom{n}{p}\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k} = 2^p\binom{n}{p}\).

Traps
  • Forgetting to say the cases are disjoint: the English answer key writes it (“These different cases are disjoint”).
  • A sum starting at the wrong index: \(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k} = 2^n - 1\).
Method 9 · DP DP 2023 ex. 4 · DP 2024 ex. 4 · lesson 6, parts 7 and 8

Total probability, Bayes, the “threshold” question

  1. Translate the statement: name the events, then write each piece of data: \(\P(S) = \dots\), \(\P(V \mid S) = \dots\), \(\P(V \mid \overline{S}) = \dots\). “If …, the probability” is a conditional probability.

  2. Law of total probability: announce the partition \(\{S, \overline{S}\}\), write \(V = (V \cap S) \cup (V \cap \overline{S})\), a disjoint union, then the formula in letters, the numbers, the simplified fraction.

  3. Bayes: “We are looking for \(\P(S \mid V)\)”, then \(\frac{\P(V \cap S)}{\P(V)} = \frac{\P(V \mid S)\P(S)}{\P(V)}\).

  4. Threshold: let \(p = \P(S)\), write \(\P(V) = ap + b(1 - p)\), solve the inequality, conclude with a proportion between 0 and 1.

Example · DP exam 2023, exercise 4: support classes \(S\), passing \(V\)

Model answer

1. \(\P(S) = 0.1\), \(\P(V \mid S) = 0.8\) and \(\P(V \mid \overline{S}) = 0.3\).

2. Since \(\{S, \overline{S}\}\) is a partition of \(\Omega\), \(V = (V \cap S) \cup (V \cap \overline{S})\) (disjoint union). Hence

\[\P(V) = \P(V \mid S)\P(S) + \P(V \mid \overline{S})\P(\overline{S}) = 0.8 \times 0.1 + 0.3 \times 0.9 = 0.35 = \frac{7}{20}.\]

3. We are looking for \(\P(S \mid V)\):

\[\P(S \mid V) = \frac{\P(V \cap S)}{\P(V)} = \frac{\P(V \mid S)\P(S)}{\P(V)} = \frac{0.08}{0.35} = \frac{8}{35}.\]

4. With \(p = \P(S)\): \(\P(V) = 0.8p + 0.3(1 - p) = 0.5p + 0.3\). We want \(0.5p + 0.3 \geq 0.75\), that is \(p \geq \frac{0.45}{0.5} = 0.9\): at least 90% of the students in the support classes.

DP 2024, exercise 4 · same plan

\(\P(C) = \frac{2}{5}\), \(\P(V \mid C) = \frac{4}{5}\), \(\P(V \mid \overline{C}) = \frac{1}{5}\). \(\P(V) = \frac{4}{5} \times \frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{5} \times \frac{3}{5} = \frac{11}{25}\); \(\P(C \mid V) = \frac{8/25}{11/25} = \frac{8}{11}\); \(0.8p + 0.2(1 - p) \geq 0.6\) gives \(p \geq \frac{2}{3}\).

Traps
  • \(\P(A \mid B)\) divides by \(\P(B)\), not by \(\P(A)\) (mistake in the official 2019 answer key).
  • Percentages: 1% = 0.01. Check that a total probability stays between 0 and 1.
  • Giving an approximate value when the paper asks for a simplified fraction.
Method 10 · DP DP 2023 ex. 6 · DP 2024 ex. 6 · lesson 7, parts 1, 2, 6 and 7

Distribution, expectation, variance, spotting a binomial

  1. Giving the distribution of \(X\) means giving \(X(\Omega)\) and each \(\P(X = x_i)\); check that they add up to 1.

  2. Show the details: \(\E(X) = \sum x_i \P(X = x_i)\), \(\E(X^2) = \sum x_i^2 \P(X = x_i)\), then \(\V(X) = \E(X^2) - (\E(X))^2\) (König-Huygens). “Do not just drop the results.”

  3. Bernoulli: \(X(\Omega) = \{0, 1\}\), \(\P(X = 1) = p\); \(\E = p\), \(\V = p(1 - p)\).

  4. Binomial: “\(Y = X_1 + \dots + X_n\) is the sum of \(n\) independent Bernoulli variables with the same parameter \(p\)”; \(Y(\Omega) = \ent{0}{n}\), \(\P(Y = i) = \binom{n}{i}p^i(1 - p)^{n - i}\); sum equal to 1 by the binomial theorem; \(\E(Y) = np\), \(\V(Y) = np(1 - p)\) (independence).

Example · DP exam 2024, exercise 6: balls 0, 1, 1, 2, twenty draws with replacement

Model answer

1. \(X_k(\Omega) = \{0, 1, 2\}\), \(\P(X_k = 0) = \frac{1}{4}\), \(\P(X_k = 1) = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}\), \(\P(X_k = 2) = \frac{1}{4}\).

\(\E(X_k) = 0 \times \frac{1}{4} + 1 \times \frac{1}{2} + 2 \times \frac{1}{4} = 1\); \(\E(X_k^2) = 0^2 \times \frac{1}{4} + 1^2 \times \frac{1}{2} + 2^2 \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{2}\); \(\V(X_k) = \frac{3}{2} - 1^2 = \frac{1}{2}\).

2. \(Y_k = 1\) if the ball shows 0 or 1, 0 otherwise: \(\P(Y_k = 1) = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{4}\). \(Y_k\) is Bernoulli-distributed with the parameter \(\frac{3}{4}\): \(\E(Y_k) = \frac{3}{4}\), \(\V(Y_k) = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{16}\).

3. \(Z = Y_1 + \dots + Y_{20}\) is the sum of 20 independent Bernoulli variables with the parameter \(\frac{3}{4}\): \(Z \sim \mathcal{B}\left(20, \frac{3}{4}\right)\), \(\P(Z = i) = \binom{20}{i}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^i\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{20 - i}\) for \(i \in \ent{0}{20}\), and \(\sum_{i=0}^{20}\P(Z = i) = \left(\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{4}\right)^{20} = 1\) by the binomial theorem. \(\E(Z) = 20 \times \frac{3}{4} = 15\); \(\V(Z) = 20 \times \frac{3}{16} = \frac{15}{4}\).

Model answer König-Huygens, DP 2023 ex. 5

By definition, \(\mathrm{Var}(X) = \E\left((X - \E(X))^2\right) = \E\left(X^2 - 2\E(X)X + (\E(X))^2\right)\).

By the formula \(\E(X + Y) = \E(X) + \E(Y)\): \(\mathrm{Var}(X) = \E(X^2) - \E(2\E(X)X) + \E\left((\E(X))^2\right)\).

By the formula \(\E(aX + b) = a\E(X) + b\) with \(a = 2\E(X)\), \(b = 0\): \(\E(2\E(X)X) = 2(\E(X))^2\). And \((\E(X))^2\) is a constant: its expectation is \((\E(X))^2\).

So \(\mathrm{Var}(X) = \E(X^2) - 2(\E(X))^2 + (\E(X))^2 = \E(X^2) - (\E(X))^2\).

The traps, topic by topic

Logic and reasoning
  • \(\neg(P \Rightarrow Q)\) is \(P \land \neg Q\), neither \(\neg P \Rightarrow \neg Q\) nor \(P \Rightarrow \neg Q\).
  • Contrapositive \(\neg Q \Rightarrow \neg P\) (equivalent) against converse \(Q \Rightarrow P\).
  • “Some” means at least one; the negation of “some fishes are red” is “no fish is red”.
  • Induction: the base case is not always 0 (\(2^5 = 32 < 37 = 6 \times 5 + 7\), whereas \(2^6 = 64 \geq 43 = 6 \times 6 + 7\)).
Sets and functions
  • \(\in\) links an element to a set, \(\subset\) two sets: \(\{3\} \subset \{1, 2, 3\}\), \(\{3\} \in \mathcal{P}(A)\).
  • \(\card(\{\varnothing\}) = 1\); \(E \times F \neq F \times E\).
  • A missing or double arrow in a diagram: it is not a function.
  • \(h \circ f = h \circ g\) does not imply \(f = g\); \(g \circ f\) only exists if the output set of \(f\) is included in the input set of \(g\).
Counting
  • “If Marie is the leader then Pierre is part of it” (Tutorial 1 ex. 3.18, 2022 test): the answer key reads “Marie leader and Pierre in it” (459), logic gives 55,692. Write your reading.
  • \((2n)! \neq 2 \times 4 \times \dots \times 2n\) (which is \(2^n n!\)); \((n+1)! = (n+1) \times n!\).
  • \(\binom{10}{7} = \binom{10}{3}\): two models may have the same answer. \(\binom{n}{k}\) is also written \(C_n^k\).
Probability and random variables
  • Incompatible (\(A \cap B = \varnothing\)) is not independent (\(\P(A \cap B) = \P(A)\P(B)\)).
  • Indistinguishable dice: the 21 pairs are not equally likely, \(\P(\text{at least one 6}) = \frac{11}{36}\).
  • Defining \(\P\) by the singletons: the sum must be 1.
  • \(\E(aX + b) = a\E(X) + b\) but \(\mathrm{Var}(aX + b) = a^2\mathrm{Var}(X)\): \(b\) disappears, \(a\) is squared.
  • A whole number “between 0 and 20”: 21 values, \(\P(X \geq 10) = \frac{11}{21}\), not \(\frac{1}{2}\).
Mistakes in the official documents: do not copy them
  • 2019 answer key: “tableau” has 7 letters, \(\frac{7!}{2!} = 2{,}520\) anagrams (not 60); \(\P_B(A) = \frac{3}{4}\) (not \(\frac{3}{7}\)); ill and not vaccinated \(\frac{0.94 \times 0.1}{0.4} = \frac{47}{200}\).
  • Seminar 4.27.3: “\(a \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} b \Rightarrow a \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} b\)”: read \(a \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} b \Rightarrow b \mathrel{\mathcal{R}} a\) (symmetry).
  • Counting summary sheet: Pascal \(\binom{n+1}{k} = \binom{n}{k-1} + \binom{n}{k}\) holds for \(1 \leq k \leq n\). Basic functions handout: \(\ln(a^n) = n\ln(a)\) only for \(a > 0\).
