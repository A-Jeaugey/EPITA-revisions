1Method 1

Switch to polar coordinates: velocity and acceleration

The most frequent chapter in the past midterms: velocity in polar coordinates (6 papers out of 7), acceleration and the circle (7 out of 7). The papers often write ρ and uρ for the course’s r and ur.

  1. The basis: r = OM > 0, θ the angle from ux to OM. ur = cosθ ux + sinθ uy and uθ = −sinθ ux + cosθ uy (turned by +90°, anti-clockwise).

  2. Its derivatives: the basis turns with the point. durdt = θ̇ uθ and duθdt = −θ̇ ur.

  3. The three vectors:

    OM = r urv = ṙ ur + rθ̇ uθa = (r̈ − rθ̇²) ur+ (2ṙθ̇ + rθ̈) uθ

  4. The circle: r = R constant, so ṙ = r̈ = 0: v = Rθ̇ uθ and a = −Rθ̇² ur + Rθ̈ uθ. Uniform as well: θ̇ = ω constant, θ̈ = 0, a = −Rω² ur, pointing to the centre.

  5. From Cartesian coordinates: r = √x² + y², and x = r cosθ, y = r sinθ give θ.

On your paper · midterm Nov. 2021 Ex 2, question 5 (2 points)

“Write the general expression of the instantaneous velocity vector and give its expression in the polar basis, explaining each step of the calculation.”

v = dOMdt (definition of velocity)= d(ρ uρ)dt = ρ̇ uρ + ρ duρdt (derivative of a product)= ρ̇ uρ + ρ duρdθ dθdt = ρ̇ uρ + ρθ̇ uθ
On your paper · TD 5 Ex 2, the merry-go-round

x = r0 cos(ωt), y = r0 sin(ωt): r = r0 and θ = ωt. So OM = r0 ur, v = r0ω uθ, a = −r0ω² ur: uniform circular motion, centripetal acceleration of norm r0ω².

Traps
  • OM = r ur, with no uθ term: θ is an angle, not a length.
  • Differentiating r ur as if ur were fixed: you lose rθ̇ uθ in the velocity, and −rθ̇² and 2ṙθ̇ in the acceleration.
  • The minus sign of uθ is on the ux component: −sinθ.
2Method 2

Write the second law on a circle

  1. Set-up: system, Earth reference frame assumed Galilean. Centre O of the circle, radius R, angle θ taken as in the statement (often from the vertical), uθ towards increasing θ.

  2. Forces drawn at M: weight, reaction (along the radius without friction), tension, friction (tangent, opposite to the motion). No “centripetal force”.

  3. Acceleration: a = −Rθ̇² ur + Rθ̈ uθ, that is v²/R towards the centre and dv/dt on the tangent.

  4. Two projections: on the radius, “forces towards the centre − forces outwards = mv²/R”; on the tangent, “tangential component = mRθ̈” (equation of motion).

On your paper · January 2018 Ex 2, ball on a sphere
on the tangent: mg sin θ = mrθ̈  ⇒  θ̈ − (g/r) sin θ = 0on the radius (towards O): mg cos θ − R = mrθ̇²  ⇒  R = m(g cos θ − rθ̇²)

3Method 3

First the speed by energy, then the reaction

  1. Height of the point M as a function of θ, from a reference point (the top, the bottom of the loop). A sketch avoids cos and sin mistakes.

  2. Mechanical energy between the start and M: the reaction does no work (perpendicular), the weight is conservative. You get v² as a function of θ.

  3. Replace v² in the radial equation of method 2. Factor out mg.

On your paper · January 2020 Ex 3, chute
V² = 2gr(1 − cos θ) (given)R = m(g cos θ − V²/r) = mg(3 cos θ − 2)

4Method 4

Leaving the support and the loop: the condition N ≥ 0

  1. A support only pushes: contact lasts as long as the computed reaction is positive.

  2. On a sphere (start at the top, no speed): N = mg(3 cos θ − 2) = 0 for cos θ = 2/3. Expected answer: θ = arccos(2/3), no calculator.

  3. In a loop of radius R, start at height h: at the top, N = mg(2h/R − 5) ≥ 0 ⇔ h ≥ 5R/2, that is v² ≥ gR at the top.

Course: chapter 2, parts 4 and 5 Practise

5Method 5

A friction force of constant magnitude on an arc

  1. List of forces: weight, normal reaction (does no work), friction tangent and opposite to the motion. The velocity is not a force.

  2. Work of friction: W = −f × length travelled; on an arc of angle θ (in radians), the length is Rθ; for a quarter circle, πR/2.

  3. Theorem: ΔEc = W(weight) + W(f), or ΔEm = W(f). Isolate f, then the numerical value.

On your paper · January 2017 Ex 3
½m(VB² − VA²) = mgR(1 − cos θ) − fRθ0.25 = 0.75 − 1.5 f  ⇒  f = 1/3 N

6Method 6

Reading a question set in the Frenet basis

Why

Six exams out of seven write their questions in the Frenet basis (uT, uN), which is not in this year’s course. On a circle, it translates word for word into the polar basis.

  1. uT = uθ (direction of motion) and uN = −ur (towards the centre).

  2. aT = dv/dt = Rθ̈ ; aN = v²/R = Rθ̇², always positive.

  3. Curvilinear abscissa: s = Rθ, and v = ds/dt: s is an antiderivative of v (June 2018: s = (V0/α) ln(1 + αt)).

7Method 7

Setting up and solving the equation of an oscillator

  1. Set-up and forces: system, Galilean Earth frame, chosen axis (origin preferably at the equilibrium position). Restoring force −k(ℓ − ℓ0) u, with the actual length of the spring and u from the attachment towards the mass.

  2. Second law, projected on the axis of the motion; if a constant term remains (vertical spring), set Z = z − ze.

  3. Standard form: coefficient 1 in front of the second derivative, ẍ + ω0² x = 0. Read off ω0 (here √(k/m)) and check its unit, s⁻¹.

  4. Solution x = A cos(ω0t) + B sin(ω0t): A = x(0), then differentiate, B = ẋ(0)/ω0. Check at t = 0.

  5. Use it: T0 = 2π/ω0, amplitude √(x0² + (v0/ω0)²), energy ½kXm², vmax = ω0Xm when passing through equilibrium.

On your paper · TD 6 Ex 2, vertical spring (z axis pointing up, O at the attachment)
mz̈ = k(−z − ℓ0) − mg  ⇒  z̈ + (k/m) z = −(g + kℓ0/m)ze = −(ℓ0 + mg/k),   Z = z − ze  ⇒  Z̈ + ω0² Z = 0,   ω0 = √(k/m)z(t) = ze − (3ℓ0/2 + ze) cos(ω0t)

8Method 8

The simple pendulum: by the second law or by energy

By the second law (TD 7, Ex 1)
  1. Polar basis at O, ur along the string; r = l constant: a = −lθ̇² ur + lθ̈ uθ.
  2. Weight: mg cos θ on ur, −mg sin θ on uθ; tension −T ur.
  3. On uθ: θ̈ + (g/l) sin θ = 0; on ur: the tension.
By energy (TD 7, Ex 2)
  1. Ep = mgl(1 − cos θ), Ec = ½ml²θ̇².
  2. EM constant (the tension does no work): differentiate with respect to time.
  3. Divide by ml²θ̇: same equation.
Then, for small angles (in radians)
sin θ ≈ θ  ⇒  θ̈ + (g/l) θ = 0,   T0 = 2π√(l/g),   θ(t) = θ0 cos(√(g/l) t)

9Method 9

Fluid friction: the equation, then the regime

  1. Add f = −αv to the list of forces; its projection is −αẋ.

  2. Standard form: ẍ + (1/τ) ẋ + ω0² x = 0, τ = m/α (in s).

  3. Regime: compare λ = 1/(2τ) = α/(2m) with ω0. Smaller: under-damped (pseudo-periodic; oscillations that die out, pseudo-angular frequency √(ω0² − λ²)). Equal: critically damped, (A + Bt) e−ω0t. Larger: over-damped (aperiodic), two decaying exponentials.

  4. Without a spring (falling drop, 2025 midterm): a single derivative, dv/dt + v/τ = −g; terminal velocity gτ, 63 % reached after τ.

Course: chapter 4, parts 4 and 5 Practise

!To avoid

The traps found in the past papers

Polar
  • No uθ term in OM.
  • duθ/dt = −θ̇ ur: the minus sign.
  • The 2 of 2ṙθ̇.
On a circle
  • “Uniform” does not mean zero acceleration.
  • The centripetal force is not a force of the list.
  • On a sphere, N ≠ mg cos θ: the term mv²/R is missing.
Energy
  • Height of M: R(1 − cos θ) below the top, R + R sin θ above the bottom of a loop.
  • Arc, not chord: length Rθ, angle in radians.
  • 10 g = 0.01 kg.
Leaving the support
  • It is the reaction that vanishes, not the speed.
  • At the top of a loop, reaction and weight add up towards the centre.
  • The expected answer is arccos(2/3), not degrees.
Oscillators
  • ω0 = √(k/m) for a spring, √(g/l) for a pendulum; T0 = 2π/ω0.
  • B = v0/ω0, not v0.
  • sin θ ≈ θ only in radians.
Waves
  • λ = c/f, not c × f.
  • Two consecutive nodes: λ/2.
  • A delay gives a negative phase shift.
The official solutions

Correct, with one approximation: in January 2018 (Ex 3), the solution takes π ≈ 3 and finds f = 1.33·10⁻² N (0.04/π ≈ 1.27·10⁻² N).
