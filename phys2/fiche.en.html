Detailed methods

In the exam

Written, answers on the paper, no calculator and no documents. Often an MCQ, then 2 or 3 exercises: a motion in polar coordinates or on a circle, an exercise on the second law and energy on a circular path, and statics (moments, absent from this year’s course). New this year: oscillators and waves, on the back.

Polar basis

ur = cosθux + sinθuy uθ = −sinθux + cosθuy durdt = θ̇uθ duθdt = −θ̇ur

r = OM > 0, x = r cosθ, y = r sinθ. Moving basis, turned by +90° (anti-clockwise).

Velocity, acceleration

OM = rur v = ṙur + rθ̇uθ a = (r̈ − rθ̇²)ur + (2ṙθ̇ + rθ̈)uθ

Circular motion

r = R: v = Rθ̇uθ, a = −Rθ̇²ur + Rθ̈uθ. Uniform (θ̇ = ω, θ̈ = 0): v = Rω, a = −Rω²ur = −v²Rur (centripetal), period T = 2πω. Cylindrical: OM = rur + zuz.

Second law on a circle

On the radius: forces towards the centre − forces outwards = mv²R. On the tangent: ΣFθ = mRθ̈ = m dv/dt. No “centripetal force” in the list of forces.

Reaction of a support

Top of a sphere (θ from the top): N = m(g cosθ − v²/R). Bottom of a bowl: N = m(g cosθ + v²/R). Without friction, the reaction does no work.

Energy, then reaction

From the top, with no speed: v² = 2gR(1 − cosθ), so N = mg(3 cosθ − 2). Leaves the support when N = 0: cosθ = 2/3 (≈ 48°).

Loop

Height of M above the bottom: R + R sinθ. N = mg(2h/R − 2 − 3 sinθ). Going all the way round: h ≥ 5R/2, that is v² ≥ gR at the top.

Friction, Frenet

Constant friction on an arc: W = −fRΔθ (radians), ΔEm = W(f).

On a circle: uT = uθ, uN = −ur, aT = dv/dt, aN = v²/R > 0, s = Rθ.

B1 reminders

ΣF = ma. ΔEc = ΣW. W(P) = mg(zA − zB). Without friction, EM is conserved.

Traps

  • No uθ term in OM; duθ/dt = −θ̇ur.
  • Uniform ≠ zero acceleration.
  • On a circle, N ≠ mg cosθ.
  • Height: R(1 − cosθ) below the top.
  • It is the reaction that vanishes when the body leaves the support.
  • Arc Rθ, not chord; 10 g = 0.01 kg.

Back · oscillators and waves

Restoring force

F = −k(ℓ − ℓ0) u

u: from the attachment towards the mass. Origin at the rest position: F = −kx ux. Stretched: the force pulls towards the attachment.

Harmonic oscillator

ẍ + ω0² x = 0 ω0 = √(k/m) x = A cos ω0t + B sin ω0t, A = x0, B = v0/ω0

Or Xm cos(ω0t + φ), Xm = √(x0² + (v0/ω0)²).

Period, energy

T0 = 2π/ω0 = 2π√(m/k), independent of the amplitude. Epe = ½kx² EM = ½kXm² vmax = ω0Xm

vmax when passing through equilibrium; v = 0 at the extremes.

Vertical spring

z axis pointing up, O at the attachment: ze = −(ℓ0 + mg/k). With Z = z − ze: Z̈ + ω0²Z = 0, same ω0. The amplitude is measured from equilibrium.

Simple pendulum

θ̈ + (g/l) sin θ = 0 T = mg cos θ + mlθ̇²

Small angles (radians): T0 = 2π√(l/g), θ = θ0 cos ω0t. Ep = mgl(1 − cos θ), Ec = ½ml²θ̇².

Fluid friction

ẍ + ẋ/τ + ω0²x = 0 τ = m/α λ = 1/(2τ)

λ < ω0: under-damped (pseudo-periodic), e−λt(A cos ωt + B sin ωt), ω = √(ω0² − λ²). λ = ω0: critically damped, (A + Bt)e−ω0t. λ > ω0: over-damped (aperiodic).

Signal, wave

f = 1/T λ = c/f = cT

Wave: a disturbance that propagates without carrying matter; c depends on the medium. s = f(t − x/c) towards increasing x. Audible: 20 Hz to 20 kHz.

PPMW, phase shift

s = A cos(ωt − kx + φ) ω = 2πf k = 2π/λ = ω/c

Δφ = φ2 − φ1 = −ωΔt (delay: negative). 0: in phase; π: antiphase; π/2: quadrature. Extra path δ: Δφ = 2πδ/λ.

Standing waves

s = 2A sin(ωt) sin(kx)

Nodes every λ/2. String fixed at both ends: λn = 2L/n, fn = nc/(2L), n ≥ 1. Closed-open pipe: fn = (2n + 1)c/(4L).

Traps

  • B = v0/ω0, not v0.
  • √(m/k) and √(l/g) in the periods, not the reverse.
  • sin θ ≈ θ in radians.
  • Amplitude ×2: energy ×4.
  • λ = c/f, nodes at λ/2.
