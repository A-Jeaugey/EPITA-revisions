Detailed methods

In the exam

Written, answers on the paper, no calculator and no documents. Often an MCQ, then 2 or 3 exercises: a motion in polar coordinates or on a circle, an exercise on the second law and energy on a circular path, and statics (not in the course yet).

Polar basis

ur = cosθux + sinθuy uθ = −sinθux + cosθuy durdt = θ̇uθ duθdt = −θ̇ur

r = OM > 0, x = r cosθ, y = r sinθ. Moving basis, turned by +90° (anti-clockwise).

Velocity, acceleration

OM = rur v = ṙur + rθ̇uθ a = (r̈ − rθ̇²)ur + (2ṙθ̇ + rθ̈)uθ

Circular motion

r = R: v = Rθ̇uθ, a = −Rθ̇²ur + Rθ̈uθ. Uniform (θ̇ = ω, θ̈ = 0): v = Rω, a = −Rω²ur = −v²Rur (centripetal), period T = 2πω. Cylindrical: OM = rur + zuz.

Second law on a circle

On the radius: forces towards the centre − forces outwards = mv²R. On the tangent: ΣFθ = mRθ̈ = m dv/dt. No “centripetal force” in the list of forces.

Reaction of a support

Top of a sphere (θ from the top): N = m(g cosθ − v²/R). Bottom of a bowl: N = m(g cosθ + v²/R). Without friction, the reaction does no work.

Energy, then reaction

From the top, with no speed: v² = 2gR(1 − cosθ), so N = mg(3 cosθ − 2). Leaves the support when N = 0: cosθ = 2/3 (≈ 48°).

Loop

Height of M above the bottom: R + R sinθ. N = mg(2h/R − 2 − 3 sinθ). Going all the way round: h ≥ 5R/2, that is v² ≥ gR at the top.

Friction, Frenet

Constant friction on an arc: W = −fRΔθ (radians), ΔEm = W(f).

On a circle: uT = uθ, uN = −ur, aT = dv/dt, aN = v²/R > 0, s = Rθ.

B1 reminders

ΣF = ma. ΔEc = ΣW. W(P) = mg(zA − zB). Without friction, EM is conserved.

Traps

  • No uθ term in OM; duθ/dt = −θ̇ur.
  • Uniform ≠ zero acceleration.
  • On a circle, N ≠ mg cosθ.
  • Height: R(1 − cosθ) below the top.
  • It is the reaction that vanishes when the body leaves the support.
  • Arc Rθ, not chord; 10 g = 0.01 kg.
