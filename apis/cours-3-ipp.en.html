Two techniques when no primitive can be read off.

Integration by parts trades one integral for another, simpler one, by differentiating one factor and integrating the other. A change of variable rewrites the whole integral with a new variable: the function, the \(dx\) and the bounds. In exams, the change of variable is always given.

The two formulas

Integration by parts. \(f\) and \(g\) of class \(C^1\) on \([a, b]\):

\[\int_a^b f(x)\,g'(x)\,dx = \left[f(x)\,g(x)\right]_a^b - \int_a^b f'(x)\,g(x)\,dx\]

Change of variable. \(\varphi\) a bijection of class \(C^1\):

\[\int_{\varphi(a)}^{\varphi(b)} f(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f(\varphi(t))\,\varphi'(t)\,dt\]

Part 1 summary § 1 · October 2022 course test

Integration by parts: statement and proof

Theorem integration by parts

Let \(f\) and \(g\) be two functions of class \(C^1\) on an interval \([a, b]\). Then:

\[\int_a^b f(x)\,g'(x)\,dx = \left[f(x) \times g(x)\right]_a^b - \int_a^b f'(x)\,g(x)\,dx.\]

With the letters \(u\) and \(v\), more common in tutorials: \(\displaystyle\int_a^b u\,v' = \left[u\,v\right]_a^b - \int_a^b u'\,v\). You differentiate \(u\) and integrate \(v'\).

Proof to know how to redo

\(f\) and \(g\) are of class \(C^1\) on \([a, b]\), so \(fg\) is differentiable and \((fg)' = f'g + fg'\).

The functions \(f'g\) and \(fg'\) are continuous on \([a, b]\) (products of continuous functions): you can integrate the equality between \(a\) and \(b\).

\(\displaystyle\int_a^b (fg)'(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f'(x)g(x)\,dx + \int_a^b f(x)g'(x)\,dx\) by linearity.

Now \(fg\) is a primitive of \((fg)'\): \(\displaystyle\int_a^b (fg)'(x)\,dx = \left[f(x)g(x)\right]_a^b\).

So \(\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)g'(x)\,dx = \left[f(x)g(x)\right]_a^b - \int_a^b f'(x)g(x)\,dx\).

In the course test

The October 2022 course test asked, word for word: “State precisely the integration by parts theorem, not forgetting its assumptions”, then “Prove this theorem”. The assumption to write: \(f\) and \(g\) of class \(C^1\) on \([a, b]\). The proof takes four lines: derivative of a product, then you integrate.

Traps
  • Forgetting the bracket \(\left[fg\right]_a^b\), or forgetting to evaluate it at the bounds.
  • The minus sign applies to the whole second integral: use parentheses if it already contains a minus sign.
  • Writing the assumption “continuous”: you need \(C^1\), since you differentiate.

Choosing \(u\) and \(v'\)

All the work is in the choice. The idea: the new integral \(\displaystyle\int u'v\) must be simpler than the original one. You differentiate what gets simpler when differentiated, and you integrate what you know how to integrate.

Method writing up an IBP
  1. Set the four functions: \(u(x) = \ldots\), \(u'(x) = \ldots\), \(v'(x) = \ldots\), \(v(x) = \ldots\)
  2. Justify: “\(u\) and \(v\) are of class \(C^1\) on \([a, b]\)”.
  3. Apply the formula: bracket minus integral.
  4. Compute the bracket, then the new integral (direct primitive or a new IBP).

Traps
  • Integrating \(e^{kx}\), \(\cos(kx)\), \(\sin(kx)\) without dividing by \(k\): \(v = \frac{e^{2x}}{2}\), not \(e^{2x}\).

Several IBPs in a row, recurrence relation

“Using one or more integrations by parts”: three situations need more than one step.

Degree-2 polynomial: two IBPs Moodle model exercise 1

Compute \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} x^2\cos(x)\,dx\).

I set \(u(x) = x^2\), \(u'(x) = 2x\), \(v'(x) = \cos(x)\), \(v(x) = \sin(x)\): \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} x^2\cos(x)\,dx = \left[x^2\sin(x)\right]_0^{\pi} - \int_0^{\pi} 2x\sin(x)\,dx = -2\int_0^{\pi} x\sin(x)\,dx\), since \(\sin(\pi) = 0\).the degree goes from 2 to 1

New IBP with \(u(x) = x\), \(v'(x) = \sin(x)\), \(v(x) = -\cos(x)\): \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} x\sin(x)\,dx = \left[-x\cos(x)\right]_0^{\pi} + \int_0^{\pi} \cos(x)\,dx = \pi + \left[\sin(x)\right]_0^{\pi} = \pi\).−π cos(π) = π

So \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} x^2\cos(x)\,dx = -2\pi\).

The integral that comes back Tutorial 3, ex. 3.12: \(M = \int_0^{\pi} e^x\sin(x)\,dx\)

First IBP, \(u = \sin(x)\), \(v' = e^x\): \(M = \left[e^x\sin(x)\right]_0^{\pi} - \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} e^x\cos(x)\,dx = -\int_0^{\pi} e^x\cos(x)\,dx\).

Second IBP, \(u = \cos(x)\), \(v' = e^x\) (we keep the same role for \(e^x\)): \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} e^x\cos(x)\,dx = \left[e^x\cos(x)\right]_0^{\pi} + \int_0^{\pi} e^x\sin(x)\,dx = -e^{\pi} - 1 + M\).

So \(M = e^{\pi} + 1 - M\), that is \(2M = e^{\pi} + 1\) and \(M = \dfrac{e^{\pi} + 1}{2}\).M reappears: we solve the equation

Recurrence relation Moodle model exercise 4

For \(n \in \N\), let \(I_n = \displaystyle\int_0^1 t^n e^{-t}\,dt\). What is the relation between \(I_{n+1}\) and \(I_n\)?

I start from \(I_{n+1}\) and set \(u(t) = t^{n+1}\), \(u'(t) = (n + 1)t^n\), \(v'(t) = e^{-t}\), \(v(t) = -e^{-t}\).

\(I_{n+1} = \left[-t^{n+1}e^{-t}\right]_0^1 + \displaystyle\int_0^1 (n + 1)t^n e^{-t}\,dt = -e^{-1} + (n + 1)I_n\).we recognise Iₙ

With \(I_0 = \left[-e^{-t}\right]_0^1 = 1 - e^{-1}\), we get \(I_1 = -e^{-1} + I_0 = 1 - 2e^{-1}\).

Traps
  • In the integral that comes back, swapping the roles in the second IBP leads to \(M = M\): keep \(v' = e^x\) both times.
  • Do not conclude “\(0 = 0\)”: when \(M\) reappears with a minus sign, move everything to the same side.
  • In a recurrence, you differentiate the power \(t^{n+1}\) (which gives \(t^n\)), never the other way round.

The change of variable

Theorem change of variable

Let \(I\) and \(J\) be two intervals of \(\R\), \(f\) a function defined on \(I\) and \(\varphi : J \to I\) a bijection of class \(C^1\). Then for every \((a, b) \in J \times J\):

\[\int_{\varphi(a)}^{\varphi(b)} f(x)\,dx = \int_a^b f(\varphi(t))\,\varphi'(t)\,dt.\]

If \(F\) is a primitive of \(f\), then \(F \circ \varphi\) is a primitive of \((f \circ \varphi) \times \varphi'\).

Official erratum of the sheet

The last line of the summary sheet writes “\(F \circ f\)”. The Moodle corrects it: read “If \(F\) is a primitive of \(f\) then \(F \circ \varphi\) is a primitive of \((f \circ \varphi) \times \varphi'\)”. It is the formula \(u' \times g(u)\) from lesson 1, read backwards.

In practice, you set \(x = \varphi(t)\), so \(dx = \varphi'(t)\,dt\), and you replace three things: the function, the \(dx\), the bounds. The course unit’s objective says so: “the change of variable always being given”.

Method five steps
  1. Write the change in the direction given: “I set \(t = \sqrt{x + 2}\)”, and justify it in one sentence (bijection of class \(C^1\)).
  2. Express \(x\) in terms of \(t\) if needed: \(x = t^2 - 2\).
  3. Compute \(dx\): \(dx = 2t\,dt\). If the statement gives \(x = \varphi(t)\), it is direct; if it gives \(t = \psi(x)\), you can also write \(dt = \psi'(x)\,dx\).
  4. Change the bounds: when \(x = -1\), \(t = 1\); when \(x = 1\), \(t = \sqrt{3}\).
  5. Rewrite everything in \(t\), simplify, compute. No \(x\) must be left.
Model answer November 2022 midterm and Tutorial 3 ex. 3.13 (4)

Compute \(K = \displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{dx}{(x + 3)\sqrt{x + 2}}\) by setting \(t = \sqrt{x + 2}\).

\(x \mapsto \sqrt{x + 2}\) is a bijection of class \(C^1\) from \([-1, 1]\) onto \([1, \sqrt{3}]\). We have \(x = t^2 - 2\), so \(dx = 2t\,dt\) and \(x + 3 = t^2 + 1\).function, dx

Bounds: \(x = -1 \Rightarrow t = 1\); \(x = 1 \Rightarrow t = \sqrt{3}\).bounds

\(K = \displaystyle\int_1^{\sqrt{3}} \frac{2t\,dt}{(t^2 + 1)\,t} = 2\int_1^{\sqrt{3}} \frac{dt}{t^2 + 1} = 2\left[\arctan(t)\right]_1^{\sqrt{3}} = 2\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{\pi}{6}\).

Traps
  • Forgetting to change the bounds: the most frequent mistake. The new bounds are the values of the new variable.
  • Replacing \(dx\) by \(dt\) without the factor \(\varphi'(t)\): with \(x = t^2 - 2\), \(dx = 2t\,dt\).
  • Leaving an \(x\) in the integral in \(t\).
  • When the new lower bound is greater than the upper one, keep the order you get: the sign takes care of itself.

What it tells you
  • IBP is almost always “degree-1 polynomial \(\times\) exponential or sine / cosine”: differentiate the polynomial.
  • The change of variable almost always leads to \(\displaystyle\int \frac{dt}{1 + t^2} = \arctan\). To know: \(\arctan(0) = 0\), \(\arctan\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = \frac{\pi}{6}\), \(\arctan(1) = \frac{\pi}{4}\), \(\arctan(\sqrt{3}) = \frac{\pi}{3}\).
  • The course test may ask for the statement and the proof of IBP (October 2022).
