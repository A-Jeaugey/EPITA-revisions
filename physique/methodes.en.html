1Method 1

Set up the study framework

The first three or four lines of your paper. The TD answer keys write them for every exercise, and TD 1 makes it a question of its own.

  1. The system. What you study, modelled as a material point M (of mass m when forces come in).

  2. The reference frame. Terrestrial, assumed Galilean, for anything moving on or near the ground. Geocentric for a satellite (TD 5 Ex 3).

  3. The coordinate system. The origin O (where?), the axes and their direction. Draw them: the sign of every component depends on them. The course points uy upwards.

  4. The origin of time. t = 0 at the launch, the release or the start. Write the initial conditions: OM(0) and v(0).

  5. In dynamics, the forces involved (method 7) come right after: the course writes them in the same box.

On your paper · TD 1 Ex 3, question 1

“Describe the study framework (reference frame, system under study, coordinate system).”

  • Reference frame: terrestrial, assumed Galilean.
  • System: the projectile, modelled as a material point M.
  • Coordinate system: Cartesian (O, ux, uy), O at the launch point, uy upwards.
  • Origin of time: t = 0 at the launch; OM(0) = 0 and v(0) = v0.
Trap

Flipping an axis flips the signs: with uy upwards, the weight is W = −mg uy and ay = −g; with uy downwards, ay = +g. Neither is wrong, but say which one you use and stick to it.

2Method 2

Differentiate the equations of motion, compute the norms

Asked in all 7 papers found. You are given OM(t), or x(t) and y(t); everything is done by differentiating.

  1. Read the equations of motion: x(t) in front of ux, y(t) in front of uy. They are numbers, with no basis vector.

  2. Differentiate each component: ux and uy are fixed. vx = ẋ, vy = ẏ, then ax = ẍ, ay = ÿ. For cos(ωt), do not forget the factor ω: ddtcos(ωt) = −ω sin(ωt).

  3. Compute the norm with Pythagoras: ‖v‖ = √ẋ² + ẏ². Factorise and simplify with cos² + sin² = 1.

  4. Check: at t = 0, do you get the initial velocity back? A norm is positive: |R|ω if R may be negative.

On your paper · midterm Nov. 2017 Ex 1

x(t) = 1 + R sin(ωt) and y(t) = 2 + R cos(ωt), with R and ω constant.

v = Rω cos(ωt)−Rω sin(ωt)a = −Rω² sin(ωt)−Rω² cos(ωt)

‖v‖ = √R²ω²(cos²(ωt) + sin²(ωt)) = |R|ω: constant, so the motion is uniform. Likewise ‖a‖ = |R|ω².

Traps
  • Writing ‖v‖ = ẋ + ẏ: the norm goes through the squares and the square root.
  • Forgetting ω when differentiating cos(ωt). The TD 5 sheet (Ex 2, Q6) makes this very mistake: its v is in metres, not in m·s⁻¹.
3Method 3

Describe the motion

A frequent MCQ question (“uniform? accelerated?”), and question 5 of the October 2022 projectile.

WordWhat it meansHow to check it
Rectilinear, circular, parabolicThe shape of the trajectoryThe trajectory equation (method 5)
UniformThe norm of the velocity is constant‖v‖ does not depend on t
AcceleratedThe speed increasesv · a > 0
DeceleratedThe speed decreasesv · a < 0
Uniformly accelerated (varied)Constant acceleration, along a linea does not depend on t

If v · a = 0 with a ≠ 0, only the direction of the velocity changes: this is uniform circular motion, and the apex of a projectile. You can also describe a single component: for a projectile, the motion is uniform along (Ox), not along (Oy).

On your paper · midterm Nov. 2021, MCQ 3

“x(t) = 10 − 2t².” Differentiate: v = −4t and a = −4 m·s⁻². So v · a = 16t > 0 for t > 0: the motion is accelerated, even though x decreases. The norm of a is 4 m·s⁻², not 2. None of the four options was right.

On your paper · midterm Oct. 2022, MCQ 3

x = 4t, y = 3t² − 6t + 5: v = (4; 6t − 6), a = (0; 6), v · a = 36(t − 1). Decelerated before t = 1 s, accelerated afterwards: plain “decelerated” is wrong. vx = 4 never vanishes, so neither does the velocity.

Traps
  • “Uniform” means neither “constant acceleration” nor “straight trajectory”.
  • Uniform circular motion: a ≠ 0 (centripetal acceleration). Uniform rectilinear motion: a = 0.
  • Judging “accelerated” from the sign of x or of v alone: what matters is the sign of the product v · a.
4Method 4

Integrate with the initial conditions

The way back: from the acceleration (given, or found with the second law) to the position. Each integration adds a constant, and the initial conditions fix it.

  1. Write a in components in your coordinate system.

  2. Integrate once: vx(t) = ∫ ax dt = … + C1. At t = 0, vx(0) is the initial velocity: it gives C1.

  3. Integrate again: x(t) = ∫ vx dt = … + C2, and C2 = x(0), the starting position.

  4. Check: set t to 0 in your results.

On your paper · TD 2 Ex 3, the robot

Newton’s second law gives a = F/m = 5 m·s⁻². The robot leaves A (x = 0) at 2 m·s⁻¹ at t = 0.

v(t) = 5t + C1, and v(0) = 2 gives C1 = 2: v(t) = 5t + 2.x(t) = 52t² + 2t + C2, and x(0) = 0 gives C2 = 0.

After one minute: x(60) = 2.5 × 3600 + 2 × 60 = 9120 m, that is 9.12 km.

The model from the course (free fall)

A point released from y0 = 5 m with no initial velocity, uy upwards: a = −g, v(t) = −gt (c1 = 0), y(t) = −12gt² + 5 (c2 = 5). It hits the ground when y(T) = 0: T = √10g ≈ 1 s.

Traps
  • Forgetting the constant: in TD 1 Ex 1, the car starts from x(0) = 7 m, and those 7 m stay in x(t).
  • Mixing up position and distance travelled: the distance is x(t) − x(0) (the official TD 1 key gives 27 m instead of 20 m).
5Method 5

The trajectory equation

A relation between x and y only: the path followed, without saying when. Asked in 6 papers out of 7.

  1. If x(t) is simple (projectile: x = v0 cosα · t), isolate t, then substitute it into y(t).

  2. If x and y contain cos(ωt) and sin(ωt), isolate them and use cos² + sin² = 1: neither t nor ω is left.

  3. Name the curve and give its features: centre and radius of a circle, semi-axes of an ellipse, apex of a parabola, ends of a segment.

On your paper · TD 1 Ex 3 and midterm Nov. 2021 Ex 3

From x = v0 cosα · t you get t = xv0 cosα, then in y(t) = v0 sinα · t − 12gt²:

y(x) = −g2v0² cos²α x² + x tanα

A downward parabola. With “5t²” in the paper (g = 10 m·s⁻²), the first coefficient becomes −5v0² cos²α.

On your paper · midterm Nov. 2017 Ex 1

sin(ωt) = x − 1R and cos(ωt) = y − 2R, so (x − 1)² + (y − 2)² = R²: a circle with centre C(1; 2) and radius |R|.

Traps
  • Leaving some t in y(x).
  • Forgetting the starting height: launched from (0, h), y(x) = … + x tanα + h (Oct. 2022).
  • Believing that y(x) tells you whether the motion is uniform: it no longer contains time (Oct. 2022, MCQ 2).
6Method 6

Apex and point of impact

Projectile motion came up in the last two papers found. Each question turns into a simple condition.

  1. The apex S: the velocity there is horizontal, vy(tS) = 0. You get tS, then xS = x(tS) and yS = y(tS).

  2. The impact P: y(tP) = landing height (0 for the ground). Keep the positive root, not the starting instant.

  3. The range: xP = x(tP). The speed at impact: ‖v(tP)‖, or faster with energy (method 8).

On your paper · midterm Oct. 2022 Ex 2, questions 8 then 7

Launched from (0, h): vy = v0 sinα − gt vanishes at t′ = v0 sinαg. Substitute into x(t) and y(t):

xS = v0² sinα cosαgyS = h + v0² sin²α2g

Question 8 (the time) is needed for question 7 (the coordinates): always read the whole exercise before you start.

With numbers

v0 = 20 m·s⁻¹, α = 30°, g = 10 m·s⁻², launched from the ground: tS = 20 × 0.510 = 1 s, yS = 400 × 0.2520 = 5 m. y = t(10 − 5t) = 0 gives t = 0 (the launch) or tP = 2 s, and xP = 20 cos30° × 2 = 20√3 ≈ 35 m.

Traps
  • At the apex, only vy vanishes: v = v0 cosα ux, not 0.
  • tP = 2tS only if the launch and the landing are at the same height.
  • The ball in TD 3 Ex 2 clears the bar if 3 < y(40) < 8 m: you compare a height, you do not look for an apex.
7Method 7

Forces involved and Newton’s second law

Never came up in the past midterms, but on the syllabus: the models are TD 2 and the free fall of the course. It is also the method behind the MCQ on the components of a force (4 papers out of 7).

  1. Framework (method 1), then the forces involved: weight W = mg (vertical, downwards), surface reaction force R (perpendicular to the surface), tension T (along the string), friction f (against the motion), driving force.

  2. Force diagram: the system is a point, each force is an arrow starting from it. Choose the axes: on an inclined plane, one axis along the slope.

  3. Write the law with vectors: ΣF = ma. At equilibrium, or at constant velocity on a straight line: ΣF = 0.

  4. Project each force on each axis, in columns. For an angle, find the adjacent side: it takes the cosine.

  5. Solve, then integrate if you are asked for v(t) or x(t) (method 4).

On your paper · TD 2 Ex 2, a robot at rest on a slope

System: the robot, point M of mass m. Terrestrial reference frame, assumed Galilean. Forces: W, R, f. Axes: ux along the slope, downwards; uy perpendicular, upwards. Equilibrium: W + R + f = 0.

mg sinα−mg cosα + 0R + −f0 = 00

Along ux: f = mg sinα. Along uy: R = mg cosα. With α = 30°: f = mg2 and R = √32mg.

MCQ · midterm Nov. 2017, question 2

F1 points up and to the right, and α is the angle between the Oy axis and F1. The side adjacent to α lies on Oy: F1 = (F1 sinα; F1 cosα). Were α measured from Ox, it would be (F1 cosα; F1 sinα).

Traps
  • Swapping sine and cosine on a slope: the angle between W and the normal to the plane is α, so the normal component is mg cosα.
  • Mixing up component and norm: in a fall, vy = −gt is negative; the speed is gt.
  • Writing f = −αv and forgetting that vy < 0 in a fall: projected on an upward axis, fy = −αvy > 0.
8Method 8

Work-kinetic energy theorem, mechanical energy

For a speed between two points, without time, energy is faster than the second law. If you are asked for a duration or a trajectory, go back to the second law.

  1. List the forces and the work done by each one between A and B: W(W) = mg(zA − zB), positive when the point goes down; W(R) = 0 if R is perpendicular to the displacement; constant friction: W(f) = −f · AB; constant force: W(F) = F · AB · cosθ, with θ the angle between F and AB.

  2. Work-kinetic energy theorem: 12mvB² − 12mvA² = ΣWA→B.

  3. Or, if only the weight does work (no friction): EM = Ek + Ep is conserved, with Ep = mgz (Ep = 0 on the ground). Say why it is conserved.

  4. Solve for vB (positive root), then the numerical value.

On your paper · TD 4 Ex 4, the circular arc

From A to B, with no friction, R does no work: mechanical energy is conserved. B is h = R(1 − cosθ) lower than A.

12mvA² + mgh = 12mvB²vB = √vA² + 2gR(1 − cosθ)vB = √9 + 16 = 5.0 m·s⁻¹

Then on BC, only friction does work: 12mvC² − 12mvB² = −f · BC, so f = m(vB² − vC²)2BC = 0.10 × 164 = 0.40 N.

Which one to use · your teacher asks for it
  • Mechanical energy: only if the only forces doing work are conservative (the weight). A normal reaction perpendicular to the displacement does no work. Then EM(A) = EM(B): one equation, no work to compute.
  • Work-energy theorem: always valid, and required as soon as a non-conservative force does work (friction, a driving force). Write the work of each force.
  • With friction, the mechanical energy decreases: its change is the work of the friction, which is negative.
  • Neither of them if you are asked for a duration, the trajectory or the acceleration: that is the second law (method 7).
The result to know

Free fall from a height h with no initial velocity: mgh = 12mv², so v = √2gh (TD 4 Ex 1). The mass does not matter.

Traps
  • Work is a number (in J), not a vector, and its sign matters: driving if positive, resisting if negative. In English, mind the letters: weight W (a vector), work WA→B (a number).
  • The angle in the work is the one between the force and the displacement, not the vertical.
  • With friction, mechanical energy decreases: do not write EM = constant.
  • TD 4 Ex 3: with the data given, the robot never reaches C (the balance gives 12mvC² < 0). An impossible result points to a mistake in the statement or in your work: say so.
9Method 9 · not in midterm 1

Switch to polar coordinates: velocity and acceleration

Not in midterm 1

Polar coordinates are not in the list of topics sent by your teacher. This method is for later.

The most frequent chapter in the past midterms: velocity in polar coordinates (6 papers out of 7), acceleration and the circle (7 out of 7). The papers often write ρ and uρ for the course’s r and ur.

  1. The basis: r = OM > 0, θ the angle from ux to OM. ur = cosθ ux + sinθ uy and uθ = −sinθ ux + cosθ uy (turned by +90°, anti-clockwise).

  2. Its derivatives: the basis turns with the point. durdt = θ̇ uθ and duθdt = −θ̇ ur.

  3. The three vectors:

    OM = r urv = ṙ ur + rθ̇ uθa = (r̈ − rθ̇²) ur+ (2ṙθ̇ + rθ̈) uθ

  4. The circle: r = R constant, so ṙ = r̈ = 0: v = Rθ̇ uθ and a = −Rθ̇² ur + Rθ̈ uθ. Uniform as well: θ̇ = ω constant, θ̈ = 0, a = −Rω² ur, pointing to the centre.

  5. From Cartesian coordinates: r = √x² + y², and x = r cosθ, y = r sinθ give θ.

On your paper · midterm Nov. 2021 Ex 2, question 5 (2 points)

“Write the general expression of the instantaneous velocity vector and give its expression in the polar basis, explaining each step of the calculation.”

v = dOMdt (definition of velocity)= d(ρ uρ)dt = ρ̇ uρ + ρ duρdt (derivative of a product)= ρ̇ uρ + ρ duρdθ dθdt = ρ̇ uρ + ρθ̇ uθ
On your paper · TD 5 Ex 2, the merry-go-round

x = r0 cos(ωt), y = r0 sin(ωt): r = r0 and θ = ωt. So OM = r0 ur, v = r0ω uθ, a = −r0ω² ur: uniform circular motion, centripetal acceleration of norm r0ω².

Traps
  • OM = r ur, with no uθ term: θ is an angle, not a length.
  • Differentiating r ur as if ur were fixed: you lose rθ̇ uθ in the velocity, and −rθ̇² and 2ṙθ̇ in the acceleration.
  • The minus sign of uθ is on the ux component: −sinθ.
10Method 10

Check the dimensions, then the numerical value

  1. The literal result first, boxed. The numerical value comes next, with its SI unit.

  2. Same unit for every term of a sum and for both sides of an equals sign.

  3. Dimensionless: whatever sits inside a cos, a sin or an exponential. ωt is an angle: ω in rad·s⁻¹.

  4. Unit of a coefficient: isolate it and replace each quantity by its unit. N = kg·m·s⁻², J = N·m, W = J·s⁻¹.

On your paper · TD 2 Ex 3, part II, question 2

f = αv, so [α] = [f][v] = kg·m·s⁻²m·s⁻¹ = kg·s⁻¹.

Quick check

v = √2gh: √m·s⁻² × m = m·s⁻¹, a velocity indeed. Rω²: m × s⁻² = m·s⁻², an acceleration. The TD 5 sheet (Ex 2, Q6) gives v = −r0 sin(ωt) ux + …: metres, ω is missing.

Traps
  • A misplaced root: √2hg, not √2hg; √2gh, not 2gh.
  • Giving a number with no unit, or a negative norm.
11Method 11

Conversions and arithmetic without a calculator

Speeds

km/h → m·s⁻¹: divide by 3.6. 36 km/h = 10 m·s⁻¹, 90 km/h = 25 m·s⁻¹, 180 km/h = 50 m·s⁻¹.

Durations

5 min 20 s = 320 s; 1 h = 3600 s. Always in seconds before computing.

Special angles

sin30° = cos60° = 12; cos30° = sin60° = √32 ≈ 0.87; cos45° = sin45° = √22 ≈ 0.71.

Gravity

g = 10 m·s⁻² when the paper says so. “5t²” in a statement is 12gt² with g = 10.

On your paper · TD 1 Ex 2, the TGV then the Formula 1

From 90 to 320 km/h in 5 min 20 s: Δv = 230 km/h ≈ 63.9 m·s⁻¹ and Δt = 320 s, so a ≈ 63.9320 ≈ 0.20 m·s⁻², about 0.02g. The Formula 1 goes from 0 to 180 km/h = 50 m·s⁻¹ in 4 s: a = 12.5 m·s⁻² = 1.25g (the official key rounds it to 12).

Powers of 10 (TD 5 Ex 3)

v = √GMTr with G = 6.67 × 10−11, MT = 6 × 1024 kg, r = 1.28 × 107 m: 6.67 × 6 ≈ 40, then 40 × 10131.28 × 107 ≈ 31 × 106, and √3.1 × 107 ≈ 5.6 × 103 m·s⁻¹. Group the mantissas on one side, the powers of 10 on the other.

!Reread the day before

The traps

Vectors
  • A vector is not a number: OM = xux + yuy, never x + y.
  • v is tangent to the trajectory, not collinear to OM.
  • Component (signed) ≠ norm (positive).
Motion
  • Uniform = constant norm of the velocity.
  • Accelerated or decelerated: sign of v · a, not of x.
  • Uniform circular motion: a centripetal, not zero.
Calculation
  • Integration constants fixed at t = 0.
  • No t left in y(x).
  • t = 0 is the launch, not the impact.
  • Factor ω when differentiating cos(ωt).
Forces and energy
  • Sine or cosine: find the side adjacent to the angle.
  • Direction of the vertical axis, sign of the weight.
  • Work done by the weight mg(zA − zB): positive going down.
  • Friction: mechanical energy decreases.
Mistakes in the official documents

Do not copy them: TD 1 key (a position given for a distance, 12 instead of 12.5 m·s⁻²), TD 5 sheet Ex 2 Q6 (factor ω missing), TD 4 Ex 3 (the robot does not reach C), course p. 5 (gravity written without a minus sign: the force is attractive, F = −Gm1m2d²u12 with u12 from 1 to 2, for the force exerted by 1 on 2), November 2021 key (MCQ 1: the right answer is c). The English course also says the velocity is collinear to “the position vector”: it is tangent to the trajectory.
