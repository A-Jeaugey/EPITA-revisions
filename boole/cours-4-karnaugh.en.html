Part 1 course § III.6.1

From the truth table to the Karnaugh map

A Karnaugh map is another way to draw a truth table: a grid with one cell per row of the table, so 2n cells for n inputs. It is a graphical simplification method: you see at a glance which terms combine.

With three inputs A, B, C: eight cells, in two rows (A = 0, A = 1) and four columns (BC = 00, 01, 11, 10).

The Gray code

The columns are not in the order 00, 01, 10, 11 but in the order 00, 01, 11, 10: the Gray code, where only one bit changes from one column to the next. That is the whole secret of the map: two neighbouring cells differ by only one variable.

Each cell contains the value of the output for its row and its column. The top-left cell gives X for A = B = C = 0; the bottom-right cell gives X for A = B = 1 and C = 0.

Trap: copying in the order of the table

The truth table is in the order 000, 001, 010, 011; the map is in the order 00, 01, 11, 10. The last two columns are “crossed”: rows 010 and 011 of the table go into columns 10 and 11. If you fill the map row by row from the table without thinking, you swap two columns and the whole map is wrong.

Part 2 course § III.6.1

Numbering the cells

If the inputs form a binary number N, each cell has a decimal number: the number of its row in the truth table. The small number at the top left of each cell below is that number.

Remember: the numbers of a 4 × 4 map

Rows 00, 01, 11, 10 and columns 00, 01, 11, 10: the first row is 0 1 3 2, the second 4 5 7 6, the third 12 13 15 14, the last 8 9 11 10. Columns 3 and 4 are swapped compared to the natural order, and so are rows 3 and 4.

The course advises writing the variables in the same order as the truth table (rows AB, columns CD): the correspondence is easier. A map with AC as rows and B as columns would be correct too, but slower to fill in.

The convention of the papers

The papers write N = (D, C, B, A), “A is the least significant bit”: the rows are DC and the columns are BA. The cell DC = 01, BA = 10 is N = 0110 = 6. The numbers are placed exactly as in the AB / CD map above.

Part 3 course § III.6.2

Grouping the 1s

To simplify, you circle groups of adjacent 1s, following four rules:

  1. The number of cells in a group is a power of 2: 1, 2, 4, 8… A group is a rectangle (2 × 2, 1 × 4, 2 × 4…), never an L or a diagonal.

  2. All the 1s, and only the 1s, are circled. A 1 can be in several circles.

  3. The number of circles is as small as possible.

  4. The size of each circle is as large as possible.

Each circle will give a product: three circles, three terms. The simplest form of X is a sum of three products.

A circle of 2k cells removes k variables: a 2-cell circle gives a product of n − 1 variables, a 4-cell circle a product of n − 2 variables. The larger the circle, the shorter the product.

Trap: circles that are too small

Circling the 1s one by one gives the minterm canonical form, not the simplest form. Always look for the largest possible rectangle around each 1, even if it overlaps a circle you have already drawn.

Reading a group

Each circle translates into a product, variable by variable, by looking at its values on all the cells of the circle:

On the cells of the circle, the variable…In the product
changes (is 0 on some cells, 1 on others)it disappears
is always 0it appears complemented
is always 1it appears as is

The simplest form obtained with a Karnaugh map uses only the basic operators (NOT, AND, OR). You can then gain even more with EXCLUSIVE-OR: ~A.~B + A.B = ~(A ^ B), so X = ~(A ^ B) + C.

Trap: reading a header line

The headers 01 and 10 do not say the same thing: for the column BA = 10, B = 1 and A = 0. Read each header in the order of the variables written above it.

Part 5 course § III.6.2

The map is a torus

Think of the map as wrapped around a torus (a lifebuoy): the leftmost column touches the rightmost column, and the top row touches the bottom row. So you can group cells on both sides of an edge, and even the four corners.

The rule behind it: two cells are adjacent when only one variable changes between them. Columns 00 and 10 differ by only one bit: they are neighbours.

Remember

Before concluding that a 1 is isolated, look on the other side of the map: on the opposite edge, or in the corners. Exam 2 of 2023-2024 had the four corners (S5 = ~C.~A).

Trap: the diagonal

Two cells on a diagonal are never adjacent: two variables change. Only sides (and opposite edges) count.

Part 6 papers 2018-2026

The undefined cells Φ

Sometimes an input combination never happens (a BCD digit above 9, a high sensor submerged without the low sensor…), or its output does not matter. The output is then undefined: you write Φ (phi) in the cell. The chapter does not introduce it, but all the recent papers use it.

The rule for Φ
  • A Φ is 0 or 1, your choice: you put it in a circle if it lets you enlarge the circle or remove one.
  • You are never required to circle a Φ, and you never circle a Φ alone.

Without the Φ cells, you would need three circles. With the Φ cells 5, 7, 13 and 15, rows DC = 01 and 11 fill up: a circle of 8 cells gives C. The remaining corners give ~B.~A. The Φ cells 3 and 10 are of no use: they stay outside the circles (so they count as 0).

Trap: circling every Φ

A useless Φ inside a circle adds cells that are not a power of 2, or one term too many. The Φ cells serve the simplification, not the other way round.

Filling in a map from the numbers

The papers give the functions by their numbers: “Let us consider N, a 4-bit binary number (D, C, B, A). A is the least significant bit. S1 = 1 iff N = 2, 3, 6, 7, 10, 11, 12 or 13”. You have to put each number in its cell: this is where most points are lost, because “no points will be given to an expression if its Karnaugh map is wrong”.

Trap: 3 bits or 4 bits

With 3 bits (C, B, A), the map has 2 rows (C) and 4 columns (BA): first row 0 1 3 2, second row 4 5 7 6. Do not put the 4 in column 10.

Part 8 papers 2014-2026

Karnaugh in the exam

The complete method, as the answer keys expect it:

  1. Draw and fill in the map, Φ included, checking the position of the numbers (0 1 3 2 / 4 5 7 6 / 12 13 15 14 / 8 9 11 10).

  2. Circle: first the 1s that can be in a large circle in only one way, then complete with as few circles as possible, each as large as possible (Φ and edges included).

  3. Read each circle: a variable that changes disappears, at 0 it is complemented, at 1 it is written as is.

  4. Write the sum of products, without parentheses, without EXCLUSIVE-OR unless the question asks for it.

  5. EXCLUSIVE-OR (if asked): spot X.~Y + ~X.Y or X.Y + ~X.~Y in the form you obtained.

Here ~D.B + ~C.B = B.(~D + ~C) = B.~(C.D), and the last circle gives D.C.~B: this is B.~Y + ~B.Y with Y = C.D, so S1 = B ^ (C.D).

In the exam
  • A Karnaugh map exercise in every paper since 2014, most often 5 or 6 points: four to six functions, most often given by their numbers, with 3 and 4 variables, with Φ cells (2021-2022, 2023-2024, 2024-2025, 2025-2026).
  • “Complete the Karnaugh maps (circles included)”: the circles must be drawn. Draw them.
  • “No points will be given to an expression if its Karnaugh map is wrong”: check every number.
  • The functions come back: S1 to S4 and S6 of 2023-2024 are those of 2021-2022.
Trap: several correct answers

There can be several minimal groupings of the same size (for example when a 1 can join two different circles). The answer keys give only one; another expression with as many terms and literals is just as simple. The site’s checker accepts all minimal forms.
