Part 1 course § I

Combinational circuit and truth table

A combinational circuit is a digital circuit whose outputs depend only on the state of the inputs at the same moment. It has no memory: same inputs, same outputs, always.

Each input and each output is in one of two states: logic state 0 or logic state 1. A combinational circuit is made of logic gates only, presented in this chapter.

You describe a circuit with its truth table: a table that gives the value of the outputs for each combination of the inputs. With n inputs there are 2n rows: 4 rows for 2 inputs, 8 for 3, 16 for 4.

The order of the rows

You count in binary, from 0 to 2n − 1. The first column is the most significant bit: it changes least often (0000 1111), the last one alternates (0101 0101).

The number of a row

Read as a binary number, a row gives its own number: with A, B, C in this order, the row A = 1, B = 0, C = 1 is row 1012 = 5. These numbers come back in chapters 3 and 4.

Key points
  • Combinational: the outputs depend only on the present inputs, not on the past.
  • n inputs → 2n rows, in binary counting order.
Pitfall: forgetting a row

A truth table with 4 inputs has 16 rows. Count them again before filling in the output: in the exams the table is often already drawn, but on scratch paper a skipped row ruins the whole Karnaugh map that follows.

The NOT, AND and OR gates

The course draws each gate with its distinctive-shape symbol. You also need to recognise the rectangular-shape symbol, very common in datasheets: a rectangle with a sign inside.

The three fundamental operators of Boolean algebra
GateEquationThe output is 1…Rectangle
NOTX = ~Awhen the input is 0 (inverter)1 and a bubble
ANDX = A.Bwhen all the inputs are 1&
ORX = A + Bwhen at least one input is 1≥1

Notation. AND is written with a dot, like a product: it is also called the product. OR is written + and called the sum. NOT is written with a bar: ~A is read “A bar” or “not A”. These words are used in chapter 3: a “sum of products” is an OR of ANDs.

Pitfall: 1 + 1

In Boolean algebra, 1 + 1 = 1: it is an OR, not an addition. OR is 1 as soon as one input is 1. Binary addition, with its carry, is another circuit (the adder, chapter 5).

Part 3 course § II.3, 5

The NAND and NOR gates

A bubble on an output inverts that output. The NAND gate is an AND followed by a NOT; the NOR gate is an OR followed by a NOT. The bar covers the whole expression.

NAND

X = ~(A.B)

The output is 0 only when all the inputs are 1.

NOR

X = ~(A + B)

The output is 1 only when all the inputs are 0.

Pitfall: one long bar is not two short bars

~(A.B) and ~A.~B are two different functions. For A = 1 and B = 0: ~(A.B) = ~(0) = 1, but ~A.~B = 0.0 = 0. The exact link between the two is De Morgan’s theorems (chapter 2): ~(A.B) = ~A + ~B.

In the exam

The NAND gate is the star of the exams: with it alone you can build all the others (chapter 2, part 9). The 2025-2026 exam asked for the schematic of the AND, OR and XOR gates using NAND gates only (exercise 5, 3 points), like exercise 2 of Tutorial 1.

Part 4 course § II.6, 7

XOR and XNOR

The EXCLUSIVE-OR (XOR) gate is 1 when its two inputs are different. It is written ⊕: X = A ^ B. Its symbol is the OR symbol with a second arc at the input; as a rectangle, it carries =1.

The EXCLUSIVE-NOR (XNOR) gate is its inverse: it is 1 when its inputs are equal. X = ~(A ^ B).

“Different”

A ^ B = 1 iff A ≠ B. Written with the fundamental operators: A ^ B = ~A.B + A.~B.

“Equal”

~(A ^ B) = 1 iff A = B. Written with the fundamental operators: ~(A ^ B) = A.B + ~A.~B.

Key points

The XNOR is a one-bit comparator: it tells whether two bits are equal. The 2023-2024 Exam 2 expected it word for word: 'A = B' = ~(A ^ B).

Pitfall: OR and XOR

The two tables differ only on the last row: for A = B = 1, OR is 1 and XOR is 0. “Exclusive” means “one or the other, but not both”.

Part 5 course § II.8, 9

More than two inputs, and bubbles

More than two inputs. AND, OR, NAND and NOR gates accept as many inputs as needed. The meaning does not change: a 4-input AND is 1 when its four inputs are 1, a 6-input OR is 1 as soon as one of them is 1.

The examples from the course (§ II.8)
GateExpression
3-input ANDX = A.B.C
4-input NANDX = ~(A.B.C.D)
4-input ORX = A + B + C + D
6-input NORX = ~(A + B + C + D + E + F)

Bubbles. A small bubble on an input or on the output of a symbol is a negation indicator: it inverts what goes through. All combinations are possible. You read the expression by complementing each input that has a bubble, then the output if it has one.

OR, bubble on A

X = ~A + B + C + D

AND, bubbles on A, C, D and on the output

X = ~(~A.B.~C.~D)

Pitfall: the output bubble

A bubble on the output puts a bar over the whole expression, not only over the last variable: ~(~A.B.~C.~D), not ~A.B.~C.~(D).

Part 6 course § II.10

Positive logic, negative logic

In positive logic, the value 1 represents the active (true) state and the value 0 the inactive (false) state. This is the usual reading: the output of an OR gate is true (1) when at least one input is true (1).

In negative logic, it is the opposite: 0 is the active (true) state and 1 the inactive (false) state.

Take an AND gate with bubbles on both its inputs and on its output: X = ~(~A.~B). Its truth table is that of an OR. The two symbols describe the same physical gate.

Reading in negative logic: the output is true (0) when all the inputs are true (0). It is a negative-AND. Reading in positive logic: the output is 1 as soon as one input is 1. It is an OR.

The negative equivalents of the gates (course § II.10)
Gate≡ negative symbolEquality
NOTNegative-NOT (bubble at the input)~A
ORNegative-AND (bubbles everywhere)A + B = ~(~A.~B)
ANDNegative-OR (bubbles everywhere)A.B = ~(~A + ~B)
NORNegative-NAND (bubbles at the inputs)~(A + B) = ~A.~B
NANDNegative-NOR (bubbles at the inputs)~(A.B) = ~A + ~B
The rule to go from one symbol to the other

Swap AND and OR, and invert every bubble: an input or output that had a bubble loses it, and vice versa. These are De Morgan’s theorems as a drawing (chapter 2, part 4).

Part 7 course § II.10

Choosing the right symbol

In the same circuit, you often mix the two notations:

  • an input or output without a bubble is active-high: 1 = active (true), 0 = inactive;
  • an input or output with a bubble is active-low: 0 = active (true), 1 = inactive.

The principle of the course: when an input or output is active-high, you draw no bubble; otherwise you draw one.

OU, bulle sur A

“X is true (1) when A is true (0) or B is true (1) or C is true (1) or D is true (1).”

AND, bubbles on A, C, D and the output

“X is true (0) when A is true (0) and B is true (1) and C is true (0) and D is true (0).”

The reset example. A circuit has an reset input that is active-high: at 1, it forces all the outputs to 0. You want an active-low reset input: a NOT gate in front is enough. Nothing is wrong, but with the usual NOT symbol (bubble on the output), you cannot see that the input is active-low. With the negative-NOT symbol (bubble at the input), the bubble is on the side of the active-low input: one glance is enough.

Pitfall: “active” does not mean “at 1”

An active-low input acts when it is 0. In a problem statement, always check whether a signal is active-high or active-low before filling in a truth table.

Part 8 course § II.11

Combining gates: reading a schematic

Every combinational circuit is obtained by connecting gates. Two rules: an input or an output can be connected to any input; an output is never connected to another output (two outputs that would impose 0 and 1 on the same wire).

Writing the expression of a schematic. Start from the inputs and write, gate after gate, the expression of each output, up to the final output. Here is the schematic of the course: click on A, B, C to see the levels propagate (green = 1).

  1. The two AND gates: A.B and A.C.

  2. The NOR of these two outputs: ~(A.B + A.C), the bar over the whole sum.

  3. The two NOT gates, then the 3-input AND: ~A.~B.C.

  4. The final OR: S = ~(A.B + A.C) + ~A.~B.C.

Chapter 2 simplifies this seven-gate schematic into S = ~A + ~B.~C: three NOT gates, one AND and one OR are enough.

In the exam

This exact schematic has come up four times: the 2014-2015, 2019-2020 and 2022-2023 exams, and the 2017-2018 resit (3 points). Three questions: “Express the output S, without simplification”, “Give the most simplified expression of S” (sometimes with the detail of the calculation), “Give a new circuit made of three NOT gates, one 2-input AND gate and one 2-input OR gate”.

Pitfall: simplifying too early

The first question asks for the expression without simplification: copy exactly what each gate does, bars included. An answer that is already simplified does not answer the question.
