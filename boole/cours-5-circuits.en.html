Part 1 course § IV

From statement to circuit

A combinational logic problem is always solved in the same order:

  1. Encode the inputs and outputs: what does 1 mean for each variable?

  2. Write the truth table of each output, row by row, rereading the statement.

  3. Simplify each output (Karnaugh map), then possibly with EXCLUSIVE-OR.

  4. Draw the circuit from the simplest expression.

The example of the course: the holds of an oil tanker. The ship has three holds a, b, c, with one sensor each: A = 1 if hold a is full (likewise B, C). A light V comes on (V = 1) if the trim of the ship is correct: all three holds full, or all three empty, or b full and the others empty, or b empty and the others full.

A single XNOR gate is enough, and B is not connected to anything: the trim does not depend on the middle hold. The table already said so, since V = 1 exactly when A = C.

Trap: translating word for word

Do not try to write the equation directly from the sentence: go through the table. Here, “b full and the others empty” gives one row (010), not an equation.

Impossible cases: Φ

When the statement involves sensors, some combinations cannot happen: they signal an error, or else the output is undefined (Φ) there. Read carefully which of the two the statement asks for.

The tank of the 2025-2026 exam. A 1,000 L tank has three sensors: C1 at 250 L, C2 at 500 L, C3 at 750 L (1 = liquid present). The lights L1 to L4 show the level (L1: less than 250 L… L4: 750 L or more); L0 comes on when an error occurs, and the others switch off.

Only four combinations are possible: 000, 001, 011, 111 (a sensor has liquid in front of it only if the ones below it do). Each one lights a single level light: L1 = ~C3.~C2.~C1, L2 = ~C3.~C2.C1, L3 = ~C3.C2.C1, L4 = C3.C2.C1, without a Karnaugh map (the paper says so). The other four combinations light L0:

The reservoirs of the 2024-2025 exam. Two reservoirs each have a high detector (A, B) and a low detector (C, D). A submerged high detector with a dry low one is impossible: the paper asks for Φ there. With V1 = 1 if both reservoirs are full and V2 = 1 if they are empty, the Φ cells give V1 = A.B, V2 = ~C.~D and V3 = ~A.D + ~B.C (paper without an answer key: answers computed by the site).

Trap: error or undefined

In the tank, the impossible combinations are not Φ: they define L0 = 1 and switch off L1 to L4 (0). In the reservoirs, they are Φ. Putting Φ in the tank would give wrong maps.

Part 3 exams 2016-2023

Transcoders

A transcoder translates a number from one code into another. The papers have given several: multiplying a BCD digit by 4 (2017-2018, and the same table in 2022-2023), multiplying by 3 (June 2018), converting natural binary to the Aiken code (2016-2017 and 2020-2021).

BCD encodes each decimal digit on 4 bits: the input N = DCBA goes from 0 to 9. The combinations 10 to 15 do not exist: Φ everywhere. The result 4 × N is written in BCD on two digits, H’G’F’E’ (tens) and D’C’B’A’ (units).

The obvious solutions

4 × 9 = 36: the tens digit never exceeds 3, so H’ = G’ = 0; a multiple of 4 is even, so A’ = 0. The paper says so: “three solutions are obvious and do not need Karnaugh maps”. An obvious solution has no operation apart from complement (A’ = 1, A’ = ~A). Here too B’ = E’: units 2 or 6 go with odd tens.

Trap: the BCD Φ cells

The last six cells (10 to 15) are Φ: do not leave them at 0. They allow, for example, the large circle D (8 cells, rows DC = 11 and 10).

Part 4 course § V.1

The adder

An adder adds two binary numbers. On n bits, it has two parallel inputs A and B, an input carry Cin, an n-bit output S and an output carry Cout. A0, B0, S0 are the least significant bits.

Example of the course on 4 bits: A = 1010, B = 1100, Cin = 1 gives 1 + 1010 + 1100 = 10111, that is S = 0111 and Cout = 1.

The 2-bit adder (June 2018). Two numbers A = A1A0 and B = B1B0, a sum S = S1S0 and a carry R, with no input carry. The table has 16 rows; Karnaugh, then EXCLUSIVE-OR, give:

S0 = ~B0.A0 + B0.~A0 = B0 ^ A0
R = B1.A1 + B1.B0.A0 + B0.A1.A0
S1 = A1 ^ B1 ^ (A0.B0)bonus question
To remember it

The least significant bit of a sum is an EXCLUSIVE-OR (1 + 1 = 10: the bit is 0). The carry is 1 when at least two of the added bits are 1. S1 adds A1, B1 and the carry from position 0, which is A0.B0.

Trap: OR and addition

Addition is not OR: 1 + 1 gives 0 with a carry of 1. That is why the sum of a position is an EXCLUSIVE-OR and the carry is an AND.

Part 5 course § V.2

The comparator

A comparator compares two n-bit numbers A and B. It has three outputs: 'A > B', 'A = B' et 'A < B', only one of which is 1. Examples of the course: A = 0110, B = 0011 gives 'A > B' = 1; A = B = 0110 gives 'A = B' = 1; A = 0110, B = 1110 gives 'A < B' = 1.

On 1 bit (2023-2024 exam)

A.~B for 'A > B'

~(A ^ B) for 'A = B'

~A.B for 'A < B'

On 2 bits (2014-2015, 2019-2020)

A1.~B1 + A0.~B1.~B0 + A1.A0.~B0

~(A0 ^ B0).~(A1 ^ B1)

~A1.B1 + ~A1.~A0.B0 + ~A0.B1.B0

On 2 bits, 'A = B' can be found without a map: the two numbers are equal if their most significant bits are equal and their least significant bits are too. The 2019-2020 paper asked for it that way (“without the help of Karnaugh maps […] using the EXCLUSIVE-OR operator”). The two other outputs need a 4 × 4 map (rows A1 A0, columns B1 B0).

Trap: the order of the variables

The 2019-2020 paper puts A1 A0 in rows and B1 B0 in columns. With another order, the cell numbers change: always copy the header of the paper.

Part 6 course § V.3

The decoder

A decoder detects which number is present on its inputs. With an n-bit parallel input, it has 2n outputs: the output Qk is 1 when the number k is on the input, all the others are 0. Example of the course: the 1-of-8 decoder (3 input lines A2 A1 A0, 8 output lines).

Each output is a minterm

Qk is 1 on a single row of the table: it is minterm number k of the inputs. Q0 = ~A2.~A1.~A0, Q5 = A2.~A1.A0, Q7 = A2.A1.A0. A decoder is therefore wired with 8 three-input AND gates and inverters.

The outline of the course mentions memory addressing as an application: a decoder selects, from an address, the only cell (or the only chip) to activate.

Trap: 2n, not 2n

3 inputs give 8 outputs, 4 inputs give 16. A “1-of-8” decoder has 3 inputs.

Part 7 course § V.4

The multiplexer

A multiplexer sends the information of one of its data inputs onto a single output line. It has an n-bit select input, 2n data inputs and one output: the selection S chooses the data bit DS that is copied to Q. Example of the course: the 8-to-1 multiplexer (3 select lines).

Q = ~S2.~S1.~S0.D0 + ~S2.~S1.S0.D1 + ~S2.S1.~S0.D2 + ~S2.S1.S0.D3 + S2.~S1.~S0.D4 + S2.~S1.S0.D5 + S2.S1.~S0.D6 + S2.S1.S0.D7

What it is for. Inside a computer, data travels in groups of bits in parallel (8, 16, 32 or 64 wires). To talk to a peripheral (printer, camera), a 64-wire cable would be impractical: a multiplexer turns the parallel link into a serial link, one bit after the other on a single wire, by stepping through the selection. USB (Universal Serial Bus) is a serial link.

Trap: select and data

The select bits are not copied: they choose. With S = 101, Q copies D5, whatever the value of the seven other inputs.

Part 8 course § V.5

The demultiplexer

The demultiplexer does the opposite: it distributes an input line D to one of its 2n outputs, chosen by the n-bit selection S. The selected output copies D, the others are 0. Example of the course: the 1-to-8 demultiplexer.

Remark from the course

If the input D is always 1, a demultiplexer behaves like a decoder: output number S is 1, the others 0. Each output is written Qk = (minterm k).D, for example Q5 = S2.~S1.S0.D.

In the exam
  • The papers from 2014 to 2026 never ask you to wire a decoder, a multiplexer or a demultiplexer. The adder and the comparator appear there as problems on 1 or 2 bits (truth table, Karnaugh map, expressions).
  • Almost every paper has a problem to put into equations (2 to 9 points): key safe, tank, reservoirs, BCD and Aiken transcoders, adder or comparator. Exceptions: 2021-2022 has none, and in 2022-2023 the truth table was given.
